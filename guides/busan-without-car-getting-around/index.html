Getting Around Busan Without a Car: Late-Night Transit, Bus Gaps & Taxi Tips

You do not need a rental car for a good Busan trip. The metro and buses make many journeys straightforward, but coastal detours, long waits and the final trip back can take more planning than the map suggests.

Updated November 22, 20267-minute readBy Go2Busan
The short version
  • Use the metro for the main corridors, then check the exact bus connection for the final stretch.
  • For outer coastal areas, check live arrivals and the route’s weekday, weekend and final-departure information before setting out.
  • Do not plan around a generic “last train” time: the final departure changes by station, direction, line and day.
  • Save your destination in Korean and have a taxi app ready before you need a late-night ride.

1. Is Busan easy to visit without a car?

For many visitors, yes. The metro is useful for moving between central districts, while buses reach neighborhoods and coastal areas that are not beside a subway station. A transportation card also makes switching between buses and the metro easier. The challenge is not getting around the city centre; it is allowing enough time for the last stretch to a beach, viewpoint or hillside neighborhood.

Think of Busan as a set of areas rather than one compact sightseeing zone. Nampo-dong, the old bookshop lanes around Bosu-dong and the 40-step area work well as a central walking day. Gwangalli is easy to pair with an evening by the beach and a café on a nearby side street. Ilgwang Beach in Gijang is a different kind of outing: plan the coastal walk and meal around the transport options available for your return, rather than assuming another bus will arrive immediately.

2. Handling long bus intervals on outer coastal routes

Bus frequency is route-specific. A route that works well in a busy district may have a longer gap between departures farther out, especially later in the day or on weekends and public holidays. Do not rely on a remembered interval, an old screenshot or the time shown for a different stop.

Check the exact stop, not just the bus number. Open the Busan Bus Information Management System and look up the stop you will board at. Check the estimated arrival, the bus direction and the route’s first and last departure information. The city’s English bus-information page is useful for route and arrival checks, although some detailed notices or stop names may still appear in Korean. You can also use NAVER Map or KakaoMap to compare the full journey and walking sections.

Build in a buffer. For an outer-coast outing, avoid scheduling a restaurant booking, tour departure or train connection immediately after the bus is due to arrive. Allow extra time for walking from the stop, waiting for the next service and finding the correct boarding point. If the live arrival estimate is missing or repeatedly changing, treat it as uncertain and consider another route or a taxi before you are under time pressure.

Plan the return before you leave. When you arrive at a beach or coastal village, check the return stop and the next few departures while you still have a connection. Save the stop as a favourite or take a screenshot of its Korean name and stop number. This is especially useful around Gijang and other places where the final walk from the stop may be longer than expected.

3. Late-night returns: check the last train and bus separately

Busan Metro does not have one universal last-train time. The final departure depends on your station, direction, line and the day’s timetable. The same is true of buses: different routes stop running at different times, and a late-night service on one corridor does not mean that every neighborhood has a late bus.

Before an evening out, check:

  • Your departure station: the last train leaving the station where you will actually board.
  • Your direction: the final train towards your destination, not the opposite platform’s timetable.
  • Your transfer: whether the connecting line or Donghae Line service will still be running when you arrive.
  • Your final walk: whether you can safely walk from the arrival station to your accommodation, including hills and poorly lit stretches.
  • Your bus connection: the last departure from the specific stop you need, not simply the route’s general operating window.

Visit Busan gives a general overview of the metro network and notes that first and last train times vary by station. Use the Busan Transportation Corporation English website or the timetable posted at the station for the exact service you plan to take.

When should you switch to a taxi? Consider it when the last useful bus has already gone, the remaining connection is too tight, the wait is long and there is no practical alternative, or the walk from the stop is steep, dark or difficult with luggage. Do not wait until the final train has departed to start looking for a ride, particularly around busy event areas where pickup points can be congested.

4. Using taxis smartly as a backup

A taxi can make a car-free itinerary much more flexible, especially for a short final leg from a metro station to a coastal destination or accommodation. It is not a guaranteed shortcut: traffic, weather, event crowds and the availability of nearby drivers can all affect pickup time and fare.

Prepare the destination in Korean. Save the full Korean address of your hotel and each out-of-the-way stop. A venue name in English may produce several search results or no exact match. Copy the Korean address from the venue’s official page, then check the pin before requesting a ride. For a hotel, keep a screenshot with its Korean name, street address and phone number.

Use an app where possible. k.ride, operated by Kakao Mobility, is designed for international visitors and supports English, Chinese including Traditional Chinese, and Japanese. Its official information says users can view estimated fares and choose from different vehicle types. Check current sign-up and payment options in the app before your trip; do not assume every overseas card or payment method will work in every situation. k.ride official website · VisitKorea: Taxi information for visitors.

Before getting in:

  • Match the vehicle plate and car details shown in the app.
  • Confirm the destination pin and estimated fare before the ride starts.
  • Use the meter or the app’s quoted fare; ask for clarification if a driver suggests a separate fixed cash price you do not understand.
  • Check the payment method in the app and keep a backup card or cash for unexpected payment issues.
  • At crowded beaches or festivals, walk to a safe, legal pickup point away from road closures rather than expecting a taxi to stop at the busiest entrance.

5. Car-free Busan checklist

  • Install or update NAVER Map and a taxi app before leaving your hotel Wi-Fi.
  • Save your accommodation and each destination with its Korean name and full address.
  • Check the exact bus stop, direction and live arrival time for outer-coast routes.
  • Look at the return journey before starting a coastal walk or dinner.
  • Confirm the last train from your actual station, in your actual direction, on the correct day.
  • Keep a realistic buffer before reservations, tours and long-distance train departures.
  • Know where a taxi can safely pick you up if public transport becomes impractical.
  • Keep your phone charged; save key addresses and route details offline or as screenshots.

Common questions

Frequently asked questions

Can I explore Busan without renting a car?

Yes. The metro and buses cover many visitor areas, and taxis can fill gaps for coastal or late-night journeys. Group nearby sights together and check the final connection for each outing.

Do buses run frequently to every beach?

No. Service intervals vary by route, time and day. Some outer coastal stops have fewer departures, so check the specific stop shortly before leaving and allow a buffer.

What time is the last subway in Busan?

There is no single last-train time for the whole network. The final departure depends on the station, direction, line and day. Check the station-specific timetable.

Can I use a taxi app without a Korean phone number?

k.ride is designed for international visitors and offers multilingual support. Check its current registration and payment requirements before relying on it for a late-night ride.

Is it easy to get a taxi at Gwangalli or a busy beach at night?

It can be harder around event crowds, road restrictions and peak evening periods. Check the pickup point in your app and be prepared to walk to a safe legal stopping area.
