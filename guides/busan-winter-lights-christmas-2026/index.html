Busan Winter Lights & Christmas 2026–27

Busan's winter events are spread across different neighborhoods. Compare the published Haeundae and Christmas Village dates with the planned Gwangbok-ro lighting period, distinguish lighting hours from event programmes, and plan an evening without crossing the city unnecessarily.

Important date note: Haeundae and Christmas Village dates are from the official city listings checked on November 15, 2026. Gwangbok-ro’s planned lighting period comes from Jung-gu’s September 8, 2026 re-notice for lighting installation and event operations. It is a procurement schedule, not the final visitor programme; dates, hours, access and performances may change. Read the Jung-gu official re-notice.

At a glance: published and planned winter dates

  • Christmas Village Busan: November 26–December 25, 2026, at Busan Cinema Center.
  • 13th Haeundae Light Festival: November 28, 2026–January 10, 2027, around Haeundae Beach, Gunam-ro and Haeundae Square.
  • Gwangbok-ro Winter Light Tree Festival: Jung-gu’s September 8 re-notice specifies a planned lighting period of December 4, 2026–January 31, 2027, 17:30–22:30. This is the lighting-installation schedule, not confirmation of every performance or visitor programme.

Haeundae Light Festival: a beach-and-downtown evening

The city calendar lists the 13th Haeundae Light Festival from November 28, 2026, to January 10, 2027. Its listed area covers Haeundae Beach, Gunam-ro and Haeundae Square. This makes it easiest to pair with a Haeundae-area dinner, beach walk or nearby stay rather than treating it as a stop on a Nampo-dong evening.

Allow time to walk between the beach and Gunam-ro, and check the district’s latest notice for lighting hours, performances, access arrangements and any weather-related changes before setting out.

Haeundae lighting hours: the official Busan event listing and the Ministry of Culture, Sports and Tourism list 18:00–23:00. The lighting ceremony is scheduled for November 28 at 18:00 on Haeundae Beach. Admission is free. A separate VisitKorea page currently shows a conflicting date range; use the newer Busan City and Ministry listings for this edition and recheck the organizer before travel.

Gwangalli M Drone Light Show: a Saturday night option

The free show runs on Saturdays at 19:00 and 21:00 during October–February. Regular drone-only performances last about 12 minutes. The first and third Saturdays are normally drone-and-laser sessions with a total sequence of about 24 minutes. The November 2026 programme lists November 21 as a laser session and November 28 as a regular drone show. December 5 and 19 are expected laser dates under the recurring rule, but wait for the official monthly programme before treating them as confirmed.

Weather, strong winds, typhoons or technical/communication issues can delay or cancel a show, sometimes on the day. Check the official notice before leaving your hotel. See the full Gwangalli drone-show viewing guide.

Gwangbok-ro Street Festa: December 5, 2026

The official event listing gives the Street Festa time as 16:00–20:00 around Gwangbok-ro. The Balcony Music Show is a separate programme; the November listing includes November 28, 19:00–21:00. Do not confuse these performance times with the winter light-tree festival, whose 2026–27 dates and lighting hours remain unconfirmed.

December 5 is also the first Saturday of the month, so the recurring drone-and-laser rule points to a possible 21:00 show at Gwangalli. However, the December drone programme has not yet confirmed the date. The two venues are in different districts and the transfer is tight after the Street Festa ends; do not promise that both can be comfortably attended. Check the live route and allow extra time.

Christmas Village Busan: a ticketed-event check matters

Busan Metropolitan City lists Christmas Village Busan for November 26–December 25, 2026, at Busan Cinema Center. Unlike an open street-light display, this is a named event at a defined venue. Check the organizer’s current admission, reservation, operating-hour and program notices before visiting; do not assume entry is free or that every program runs every day.

Its location in Centum City makes it practical to combine with an indoor shopping or dining plan. If you also want the Haeundae Light Festival, check the evening schedule and allow for the transfer rather than assuming the two venues are next door.

Gwangbok-ro winter lights: planned dates, not the full programme

Busan Jung-gu’s September 8, 2026 re-notice for lighting installation and event operations specifies a planned lighting period of December 4, 2026–January 31, 2027, 17:30–22:30. The listed area covers Gwangbok-ro, Gwangbokjungang-ro and hillside-road sections in Bosu-dong, Daecheong-dong and Yeongju-dong. This is a procurement and operating schedule, not a complete visitor programme or confirmation of a lighting ceremony, performances, admission arrangements or parking. The notice allows for changes, so check the district’s latest public announcement before travelling. Open the Jung-gu official re-notice.

For a shorter old-town evening, combine the Gwangbok-ro section with BIFF Square, Gukje Market or Bupyeong Kkangtong Market. The Sanbok-ro sections are separate hillside neighborhoods rather than one continuous flat walk from Nampo; choose the section that fits your time and mobility. Check each market’s closing time and do not assume you can comfortably add Haeundae to the same short evening.

Which winter event should you choose?

  • For a beach atmosphere: choose Haeundae and build the evening around the beach, Gunam-ro and nearby restaurants.
  • For a venue-based Christmas outing: check the Christmas Village ticket and program details at Busan Cinema Center.
  • For old-town shopping and street food: use the planned Gwangbok-ro lighting window as a guide, then check the final district programme and keep Nampo-dong and Haeundae on separate evenings.

Practical winter planning

Coastal wind can make an evening feel colder than the daytime forecast suggests. Wear layers, bring a wind-resistant outer layer and comfortable shoes, and keep an indoor alternative in mind. Crowds can affect walking speed and public transport access; follow local staff instructions and check transit notices after major events.
