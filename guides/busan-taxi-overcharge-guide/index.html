Busan Taxi Tips: How to Avoid Overcharging

Most taxi rides in Busan are straightforward. A few simple checks—using the meter, confirming the vehicle type and keeping the receipt—can help you avoid confusion and deal with a fare dispute.

BUSAN, SOUTH KOREA6 MIN READCHECK CURRENT FARES

Quick answer

Use a metered taxi, check the vehicle type and keep the receipt. If the fare looks wrong, record the taxi details and contact Korea's 1330 Tourist Complaint Center.

The short version: Use a metered taxi, check the vehicle type and keep the receipt. If the fare looks wrong, record the taxi details and contact Korea's 1330 Tourist Complaint Center.

1. Check that the meter is running

For an ordinary medium-sized taxi, Busan's published fare is a base fare of ₩4,800 for the first 2 km. The meter then adds distance and time charges. The official city fare page lists ₩100 per 132 m and ₩100 per 33 seconds when the vehicle is moving at 15 km/h or slower. These figures are a reference for standard taxis, not deluxe, jumbo or pre-booked tourist vehicles.

Before the taxi moves, check that the meter is on and starts from the normal base fare. If a driver proposes a fixed price instead, ask whether this is an official pre-booked or charter service and confirm the full price before agreeing. Do not treat a quoted cash amount as a metered fare.

2. Know when a late-night surcharge applies

Busan applies a late-night surcharge from 23:00 to 04:00, based on the time the taxi is operating: 20% from 23:00 to midnight, 30% from midnight to 02:00, and 20% from 02:00 to 04:00. A trip crossing one of these time bands may have different portions charged at different rates. The official fare page also lists an out-of-area surcharge when a taxi travels beyond Busan's service area.

Deluxe, jumbo and tourist taxis use different fare structures. Check the vehicle type and fare information before boarding rather than comparing every taxi with the standard medium-sized taxi rate.

3. Warning signs worth checking

Be cautious if a driver refuses to use the meter for an ordinary street-hail ride, enters an unexplained amount manually, asks for a large round sum without explaining the service, or quotes a price in a foreign currency without a clear booking agreement. These are reasons to pause and ask for clarification—not proof by themselves that a driver has committed a violation.

A higher-than-expected fare is not automatically an overcharge. Traffic, route choice, tolls, late-night surcharges and the taxi category can affect the total. Compare the route and the meter reading, not just the journey time.

4. Before and during the ride

  • Use an official taxi stand at the airport, ferry terminal, station or hotel, or request a ride through a service you understand.
  • Show the destination's Korean name and address. A saved map pin can prevent confusion between places with similar English names.
  • Check the taxi category and confirm the meter is running before setting off.
  • Follow the route on your map app. If the route changes, ask politely why; roadworks, one-way streets and traffic can require a detour.
  • At the end, compare the meter total with the amount requested and ask for a receipt.

5. Keep the receipt and record the details

Ask for a receipt before leaving the vehicle. Keep the receipt or payment record, and note the date, time, pickup and drop-off points, taxi number or registration, the displayed fare and any extra amount requested. If you feel unsafe, prioritise getting to a safe public place; do not prolong an argument inside the vehicle.

Useful Korean phrases: 미터기 켜 주세요. (Please turn on the meter.) · 영수증 주세요. (Please give me a receipt.) · 이 금액이 맞나요? (Is this amount correct?)

6. How to report a taxi problem

The Korea Tourism Organization's 1330 Tourist Complaint Center accepts reports about illegal conduct, overcharging and rude behaviour. Call 1330 from within Korea or +82-2-1330 from overseas. You can also submit a complaint at touristcomplaint.or.kr. The service supports English, Chinese, Japanese and other languages. Submit the receipt, taxi details, route, time and a clear description of what happened.

For immediate danger or an emergency, use Korea's emergency services rather than waiting for a tourism complaint process. A complaint is for documenting and reviewing a travel-service problem; it is not an instant refund channel.
