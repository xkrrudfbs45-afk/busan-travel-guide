Busan Coffee Guide: Start with MOMOS, Then Explore the City

Busan is more than seafood markets and seaside cafés. Its port-city history and specialty coffee community make a coffee stop a worthwhile part of a trip. Here is how to experience one of the city's best-known local names without turning your day into a cross-city café crawl.

Our first recommendation: MOMOS COFFEE. It began in Busan in 2007 and grew into one of Korea's best-known specialty coffee roasters. Choose the Oncheon flagship for its original neighborhood setting, or the Yeongdo Roastery & Coffee Bar to connect coffee with the atmosphere of Busan's old harbor district.

Why coffee belongs on a Busan itinerary

Busan's coffee story is closely tied to its role as a major port. The Korea Tourism Organization notes that more than 90% of Korea's imported green coffee beans are processed through the Port of Busan, and highlights the city's strong café scene. That port connection is one useful way to understand why coffee feels at home in this city; it is not the only reason to visit.

The local specialty scene also has internationally recognized names. MOMOS COFFEE says it was founded in Busan in 2007. Its co-CEO and barista Jeon Jooyeon became the first Korean to win the World Barista Championship in 2019. The Busan Metropolitan Government reported in 2026 that MOMOS placed 22nd on the World's 100 Best Coffee Shops list.

MOMOS COFFEE: which location should you choose?

Oncheon Flagship Store — for the original MOMOS experience

The Oncheonjang flagship is the natural choice if you want to visit the café where the brand began. Korea Tourism Organization describes its garden seating, roasting room and bakery, and notes that it is close to Oncheonjang Station on Subway Line 1. This is a good stop to pair with a relaxed visit to the Oncheonjang area rather than squeezing it between far-apart beach attractions.

  • Address: 20, Osige-ro, Geumjeong-gu, Busan (부산광역시 금정구 오시게로 20)
  • Nearest station: Oncheonjang Station, Busan Metro Line 1
  • Official listing: Korea Tourism Organization — MOMOS Coffee Main Branch

Yeongdo Roastery & Coffee Bar — for coffee and harbor atmosphere

Choose Yeongdo if you want the roastery experience in a port-side neighborhood. MOMOS describes this space as a place where visitors can get closer to the work of roasters and baristas, from green coffee to the finished cup. Its location in Bongnae-dong makes it a natural coffee stop when exploring Yeongdo's harbor scenery and nearby neighborhoods.

  • Address: 160, Bongnae-naru-ro, Yeongdo-gu, Busan
  • What makes it different: a roastery and coffee bar, not simply another branch café
  • Official location and hours: MOMOS COFFEE — Yeongdo

Marine City and Domoheon — convenient alternatives

If your accommodation or sightseeing plans are already around Haeundae, Marine City may be the more convenient MOMOS stop. The Domoheon location offers another option around the Suyeong and Nam-gu side of the city. Check the brand's current location page for each branch's details before setting out.

What should you order?

If you enjoy tasting coffee rather than only ordering a familiar latte, look at the day's pour-over or single-origin options. MOMOS lists blends, single-origin coffee and drip bags among its offerings. Ask the barista what is tasting especially good that day; availability and beans change. If you prefer milk-based drinks, espresso and Americano options are also listed by the Korea Tourism Organization.

For a gift that travels easily, consider drip bags or packaged beans from the official store. Check current stock, roast dates and baggage rules before buying larger quantities.

Make coffee part of the neighborhood, not a detour

  • Oncheonjang day: visit the Oncheon flagship as a dedicated coffee stop, then explore nearby local streets or plan a meal in the Geumjeong area.
  • Yeongdo day: connect the roastery with a harbor-side walk and other Yeongdo sights. Leave enough time to explore the neighborhood rather than rushing straight back across town.
  • Haeundae stay: consider Marine City if it fits your existing route. Avoid crossing the city solely to tick off several branches of the same brand.

More than one famous café

MOMOS is a strong starting point, not the whole story. Busan has independent cafés and roasters across neighborhoods, from old downtown and Jeonpo to Gwangalli and Gijang. We are building this guide carefully: a café should earn a place through its coffee, local identity, setting or usefulness to a visitor—not simply because it looks good in a photo.
