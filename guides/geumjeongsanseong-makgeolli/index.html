LOCAL CULTURE · TRADITIONAL BREWING

Geumjeongsanseong Makgeolli: Busan’s Tangy Traditional Rice Wine

A gentle tang, a grainy fragrance and a brewing tradition rooted in the mountains above Busan: Geumjeongsanseong Makgeolli is more than a local drink.

Why this makgeolli tastes different

The brewery describes its makgeolli as an 8% rice wine made with locally produced traditional nuruk and underground rock water. Its flavour is often described as tangy, savoury and grain-forward rather than simply sweet. As a live-fermented drink, flavour can change with storage and age; keep it chilled and follow the bottle’s instructions.

The story of traditional nuruk

Nuruk is a fermentation starter made from grain. At Geumjeongsanseong, the brewery continues a traditional foot-pressing method for shaping wheat-based nuruk. The brewery’s own history says the practice is associated with local households going back centuries, while also acknowledging that the exact origin of the drink is uncertain. Present the long tradition as local heritage, not a precisely documented founding date.

A mountain village and its water

The brewery sits in Geumseong-dong, in the Geumjeongsanseong area at roughly 400 metres above sea level. The producer highlights the area’s climate, traditional nuruk and underground water as part of the drink’s identity. That connection to place makes it a useful story for travellers interested in food heritage beyond Busan’s beaches.

Can visitors try a brewing experience?

The official brewery page lists experiences such as pressing nuruk, making makgeolli and straining fermented rice wine. Advance booking is required; group-size rules, availability, prices and operating days can change, so contact the brewery before building a trip around a session. Official details: https://sanmak.kr/bbs/content.php?co_id=sub31

How to taste it

Chill the bottle, gently check the producer’s serving guidance, and expect a more noticeable acidity than in sweeter commercial styles. The drink may separate, so follow the label’s instructions for gently mixing it. It pairs naturally with savoury Korean dishes such as pajeon or duck dishes, but alcohol is for adults of legal drinking age only. Never drink and drive.
