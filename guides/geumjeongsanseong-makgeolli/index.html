Geumjeongsanseong Makgeolli: Busan’s Tart Traditional Rice Wine

Not every makgeolli is sweet and easy-drinking. Made in a mountain village above Busan with a distinctive traditional nuruk starter, Geumjeongsanseong Makgeolli is known for its fuller texture and noticeably tangy finish.

Flavor note: Official Visit Busan tourism information describes Geumjeongsanseong Makgeolli as thick-bodied and sour or tart, with high acidity. If you expect a mild, sweet rice drink, the sharper flavor may surprise you.

What makes this makgeolli different?

Geumjeongsanseong Makgeolli is produced in Geumjeongsanseong Village on the slopes of Geumjeongsan Mountain. The producer describes using handmade traditional nuruk (a fermentation starter), local rock-filtered water and Korean rice. Traditional foot-treading is part of the nuruk-making method. The brewery’s own description lists the drink at 8% alcohol by volume; check the current bottle label because product details can change.

Its flavor is not simply “gone sour.” The tartness is part of its recognizable style, alongside a grainy, savory aroma and a thicker mouthfeel than some sweeter commercial makgeolli. As with other unpasteurized, live-fermented makgeolli, flavor can change with storage and fermentation, so keep it chilled and follow the label’s storage guidance.

A tradition rooted in the mountain village

The producer says the precise origin is uncertain, while tracing the local nuruk tradition back to earlier centuries. The drink became more widely known as people came to the area during the construction of Geumjeongsanseong Fortress in the early 18th century. In 1980, it was designated Korea’s first folk liquor under the traditional folk-liquor system, according to the producer’s history.

That story is what makes it more than a bottle to pick up as a souvenir: the fermentation method, the mountain setting and the food culture of the village are connected. For the most reliable account of the production method and history, see the producer’s official story.

What do locals eat with it?

Geumjeongsanseong Village is known for traditional food restaurants. Geumjeong-gu’s official tourism guide highlights duck dishes, black-goat bulgogi, acorn jelly and local makgeolli among the area’s specialties. For a lighter meal, restaurants around the wider Beomeosa area also commonly feature mountain-vegetable bibimbap and pajeon; menus vary by establishment.

  • Duck bulgogi (오리불고기): marinated duck cooked with vegetables; often ordered for a group.
  • Mountain-vegetable bibimbap (산채비빔밥): rice with seasonal mountain greens and side dishes.
  • Pajeon (파전): savory scallion pancake, a familiar pairing with makgeolli.
  • Acorn jelly (도토리묵): a cool, lightly seasoned side dish found at some traditional restaurants.

Makgeolli is alcoholic. If you are driving, cycling or hiking back down the mountain, do not drink; plan a safe return and check transportation before ordering.

How to plan a visit

Geumjeongsanseong Village and Beomeosa Temple are both in the Geumjeong area, but they are separate stops and should not be treated as a quick walk from one another. Plan the temple and mountain-village meal as separate parts of your day, and check a live map for the best current public-transport route or taxi transfer.

The official Geumjeong-gu tourism page lists the Geumjeongsanseong Makgeolli brewery at 453 Sanseong-ro, Geumseong-dong, Geumjeong-gu, Busan and gives 051-517-0202 for enquiries about experiences. Brewing and nuruk-making experiences may have fees and schedules that vary, so contact the brewery before going. See the official Geumjeong tourism experience page.

A note for first-time drinkers

If possible, try a small amount with food before buying a bottle. Its tartness and fuller texture are the point, but they are not for everyone. Ask staff how the bottle should be stored and whether it needs gentle mixing before pouring. Do not shake a pressurized bottle vigorously, and follow the label.
