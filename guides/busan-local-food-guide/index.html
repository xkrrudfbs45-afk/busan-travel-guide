What to Eat in Busan: A Practical Local Food Guide

Start with the dishes Busan is known for, then explore food streets and neighborhood restaurants at a pace that fits your trip.

Quick answer: First-time visitors can start with dwaeji-gukbap (pork soup with rice), milmyeon (wheat noodles in a chilled or warm broth), Busan-style fish cakes and ssiat hotteok (seed-filled sweet pancakes). Choose food stops by neighborhood so meals fit naturally into your sightseeing route.

1. Four Busan foods to try

Dwaeji-gukbap — pork soup with rice

A hearty soup served with rice, pork and seasonings such as chives. You can usually adjust the flavor at the table. If you prefer a milder taste, add seasonings gradually and ask what is included before ordering.

Milmyeon — Busan-style wheat noodles

Chewy wheat noodles served in a chilled broth or mixed with a spicy sauce. It can be a refreshing meal on a warm day, and many restaurants offer different broth and sauce styles.

Busan fish cakes (eomuk)

Fish cakes are a familiar Korean street-food staple, and Busan has a strong association with them. Try them as a quick snack near markets or busy shopping streets; check ingredients if you have seafood allergies.

Ssiat hotteok — seed-filled sweet pancake

A warm, sweet pancake filled with a mixture that commonly includes seeds and nuts. It is often associated with the Nampo-dong and BIFF Square area. Check ingredients if you have a nut allergy.

2. Where to explore for food

  • Seomyeon: A convenient base for casual restaurants and a meal after shopping or transferring across the city. The area is also known for its dwaeji-gukbap alley.
  • Nampo-dong and BIFF Square: Useful for combining market browsing, street snacks and sightseeing around Jagalchi and Gukje Market.
  • Jagalchi and the waterfront: Consider this area for seafood-focused meals. Confirm menu prices and whether side dishes or cooking charges are included before ordering.
  • Haeundae: Convenient for pairing a meal with the beach, coastal walks and nearby attractions. Restaurants close to major sights may be more visitor-oriented, so compare menus before sitting down.

3. Two restaurant starting points

These examples are listed by the official Visit Busan tourism site. They are starting points, not a ranking; choose based on your route and confirm details before you go.

Songjeong Samdae Gukbap — Seomyeon

A long-running option for dwaeji-gukbap in Seomyeon’s pork-soup area. The official listing describes pork soup with rice and several variations, including sundae and offal options. The listing currently shows 24-hour opening, with holiday exceptions.

Ilmi Milmyeon — Nampo area

A milmyeon option in Jung-gu that can be paired with exploring Nampo-dong and nearby markets. The official listing currently shows Monday–Saturday, 11:00 a.m.–7:00 p.m., and Sunday closure; schedules can change.

4. How to choose a good local place

  • Check the most recent opening hours and weekly closing day before travelling across town.
  • Read the menu and total price before ordering, especially for seafood sold by weight.
  • Look for a focused menu and recent reviews rather than relying on a single rating.
  • During peak meal times, have a nearby alternative in mind in case there is a queue.
  • If you have dietary restrictions, ask about broth, seafood, nuts and other ingredients directly.
