Is the Visit Busan Pass Worth It?

Compare the pass options, understand when the clock starts, and decide whether the attractions you actually want to visit justify the price.

Quick answer: The Visit Busan Pass can be good value if you plan to visit several participating paid attractions within the pass period. It is less compelling if your itinerary is mostly beaches, markets, neighborhoods and other free activities. Build your route first, then compare the attractions’ individual prices with the pass price.

What is the Visit Busan Pass?

The official Visit Busan Pass website describes the pass as a product for international visitors that combines access to participating attractions and discounts at selected businesses. The attraction list and benefits can change, so use the official list to check the places you are interested in rather than relying on an old roundup.

The main choices include time-based 24-hour and 48-hour passes, plus Big 3 and Big 5 passes that cover a set number of selected attractions. Some versions are physical cards and others are mobile passes. Their benefits are not identical, especially for public transport.

Which type fits your itinerary?

Choose a 24-hour pass for one well-planned day

This can suit a day focused on attractions such as observation decks, museums, rides or cruises. Put the most important stop first, check opening hours and ticketing arrangements, and avoid building a schedule that depends on crossing the city several times. A pass does not remove travel time, queues or the need to reserve time slots where required.

Choose a 48-hour pass only if both days are realistic

The 48-hour option costs more, so estimate the value of the attractions you can comfortably visit across two days. Group places by area and leave room for meals, weather and transit. Do not count a discounted attraction as if it were automatically free.

Consider Big 3 or Big 5 for a short list of must-dos

At the time checked, Big 3 was listed at ₩45,000 and Big 5 at ₩65,000. These passes use designated attraction groups: Big 3 requires one selection from Group A and two from Group B; Big 5 requires two from Group A and three from Group B. Check the current group list and rules before deciding that your preferred combination qualifies.

When does the pass become active?

For 24-hour and 48-hour passes, the official instructions say the time period starts when you issue a ticket at your first participating attraction, not simply when you buy the pass. For example, a 24-hour pass first used at 9:00 a.m. is described as valid for pass benefits until 8:59 a.m. the following day.

That makes the first attraction an important planning choice. Avoid activating the pass late in the day if you could instead start early the next morning. The Big 3 and Big 5 products follow attraction-count rules rather than the same 24/48-hour format; check the specific validity terms for the version you buy.

Does it cover public transportation?

Do not assume every version works as a transit card. According to the official instructions, the physical card can be topped up and used like a transport card on supported public transport. The mobile pass itself does not have transport-card functionality. If you buy a physical card online, you may need to collect it at a designated exchange location before visiting attractions.

For metro and bus travel, budget separately unless you have confirmed that the physical card version you hold is set up and topped up for transport. See our guide to using public transportation in Busan for the basics.

A simple way to check whether it is worth it

  1. List the attractions you genuinely want to visit. Start with your own interests, not the pass’s promotional list.
  2. Check each attraction’s current standalone admission price. Use the official attraction or pass listing and note any reservation requirements.
  3. Group stops geographically. Estimate realistic travel time and leave room for queues, meals and breaks.
  4. Compare like with like. Add only the admission value you can realistically use, then compare it with the pass price and any separate transport costs.
  5. Check the rules before paying. Confirm eligibility, attraction group, ticket issuance, opening hours, collection point and refund conditions.

Common mistake: Buying the pass first and then trying to fill every hour to “get your money’s worth.” If you prefer slow mornings, markets, cafes, beaches or wandering neighborhoods, individual tickets may be a better fit.
