MOUNTAIN FOOD · GEUMJEONG-GU

What to Eat in Geumjeongsanseong Village: Black Goat, Duck and Makgeolli

Busan tourism information highlights black-goat bulgogi, duck dishes and traditional Sanseong makgeolli in this mountain-village food area. Restaurant menus and serving sizes vary, so confirm current details before visiting.

Black-goat bulgogi

Busan tourism lists black-goat bulgogi among the dishes associated with the Geumjeongsanseong Village food area. Ask the restaurant how the meat is prepared, how many people a portion serves and whether a minimum order applies.

Duck bulgogi and duck soup

The same tourism listing names duck bulgogi and duck soup. Menus, serving sizes and availability vary by restaurant, so confirm the day's menu and total price before ordering.

Geumjeongsanseong makgeolli

Geumjeongsanseong Makgeolli is a traditional rice wine associated with the village. Busan City describes it as a local specialty made with domestic rice and traditional fermentation. If you drink, plan your return transport first; choose a non-alcoholic drink if you are driving.

What to check before ordering

Ask whether portions are intended for sharing, whether a minimum order applies, and whether side dishes or cooking charges are included. This guide does not quote current menu prices because restaurant prices and portions can change.

Plan the visit

Geumjeongsanseong Village is a mountain-side destination rather than a quick stop between downtown attractions. Check transit, return options, restaurant hours and group reservations before you go. For a makgeolli-making experience, confirm booking rules and current availability directly with the producer.

How to reach Geumjeongsanseong Village

Official pages do not use identical stop names. The Korean Busan Geopark guide lists bus 203 from the Oncheonjang area to “South Gate” or “East Gate”, and bus 90 from Beomeosa Station to “Beomeosa ticket office”. The Geumjeong-gu English guide lists bus 203 to the East Gate entrance or Sanseong Pass, and bus 90 to Beomeosa Temple. The English Geopark page gives the main East Gate–North Gate–Beomeosa hiking course without those detailed stop names.

Use the stop that matches your planned trail, confirm the current stop name and departure in a live transit app or official notice, and check the return service before setting out. The Beomeosa approach includes an uphill walk; walking time and difficulty depend on your route and the weather.
