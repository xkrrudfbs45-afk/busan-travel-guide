GETTING AROUND

Busan Public Transportation: Cards, Metro & Buses

A straightforward guide to paying for rides, making transfers, and choosing the right transport option for your trip.

BUSAN, SOUTH KOREA7 MIN READCHECK CURRENT RULES

Quick summary

  • Use a transit card for routine metro and bus journeys, and check the current rules before you travel.
  • Plan transfers around the nearest station to each destination—not only the total journey time.
  • Check the last service for late trips and keep a payment backup for unexpected changes.

Quick answer: For most visitors making several trips, a rechargeable transport card is a convenient default for ordinary metro and city-bus journeys. Compare tourist passes against your actual itinerary before buying one, and check whether airport or special services are included.

Metro or bus: which should you use?

Choose transport by neighborhood: For Nampo, Jagalchi and much of the old downtown, the metro is a useful starting point, but steep streets and the walk between stops still matter. In western Busan, especially Gangseo and riverside areas such as Samnak Ecological Park, the metro reaches the broader area but not every destination; a taxi for the last stretch or a car can make the day much easier. In eastern Busan, including Haeundae, Centum City and Gwangalli/Suyeong, weekend traffic and parking can be frustrating, so metro, bus and walking are often a better combination than driving. Check the door-to-door route, not just whether a metro station exists.

Busan’s metro is often the simplest way to travel between major areas, while buses can bring you closer to places that are farther from a station or located on hills. For some destinations, combining the two is the most practical option. Check the full route and walking distance rather than choosing by straight-line distance alone.

For late arrivals, check the final service before you travel. A route that works in the afternoon may not be available after midnight. Our Gimhae Airport late-arrival guide covers the main options for getting to your accommodation.

T-money, Cashbee and other transport cards

A rechargeable transport card can make ordinary bus and metro trips easier because you tap it at the reader instead of buying a separate ticket for each ride. T-money and Cashbee are familiar examples, but availability, sales points and top-up options can vary. Follow the instructions at the point of sale and check the card’s current terms.

Remember that a transport card is not the same thing as a tourist attraction pass. A card’s ability to pay for rides, a pass’s included attractions, and any special transport benefits are separate conditions that should be checked individually.

Cashless buses: check your route before boarding

Busan began a three-month cashless-bus pilot on October 1, 2026. It covers 542 buses on 35 routes, including seated routes 1001 and 1003. On these buses, cash is not collected in the usual way: under the city’s “Board First, Pay Later” policy, cash-only passengers may board and settle the fare later by bank transfer.

Visitor advice: do not rely on the bank-transfer workaround if you do not have a Korean bank account. Load a T-money or Cashbee transport card before your first bus ride, and tap when boarding and leaving. The city has described the pilot as a three-month trial; check for a follow-up notice after December 2026 before assuming the same routes or rules continue.

What about WOWPASS?

WOWPASS combines a prepaid payment function with a separate T-money transport function. Do not assume the two balances are interchangeable: the transport balance is managed separately from the WOWPASS spending balance. The app may offer supported ways to top up T-money, but the available method and conditions can change, so check the current official instructions before relying on it.

Read the official WOWPASS guide and the Korea Tourism Organization’s WOWPASS information for current details.

Paying on city buses with a QR code

Busan introduced ZeroPay QR payments on city buses on September 21, 2026. This is an alternative payment method for city buses only; it does not replace a rechargeable transport card for the metro.

Look for the multilingual QR instructions inside the bus. Open a supported payment app, scan the code, complete the payment and show the confirmation screen to the driver. Busan City’s notice lists WeChat Pay, UnionPay, Alipay+ and Taiwan’s All Pay (全支付) among the services shown on its payment materials. App availability can depend on your account and region, so check that your app can make the payment before boarding.

  • The QR fare is ₩1,700 per passenger, including children and teenagers; children under 6 ride free.
  • Each passenger must scan and pay separately.
  • QR payments do not receive the transfer discounts available with eligible transport cards.
  • If you plan to transfer between a city bus and the metro, a rechargeable transport card is still the more useful option.

See Busan Metropolitan City’s official ZeroPay bus-payment notice for the current instructions.

Bus and metro transfers: the detail that matters

Busan offers transfer benefits on eligible journeys, but you need to follow the applicable rules. The city’s visitor guide describes a general 30-minute transfer window and up to two transfers for eligible journeys. Use the same eligible transport card, tap when boarding and tap off when leaving a bus. Eligible modes and exceptions depend on the current rules.

  • Tap your card on boarding and tap off when exiting a bus.
  • Check the transfer time limit and eligible modes before your journey; the city’s visitor guide describes a general 30-minute transfer window and up to two transfers.
  • Do not assume airport limousine services or every special service qualify for the same transfer discount.
  • Cash payments may not qualify for the same transfer benefits as an eligible transport card.

Because transfer terms can be revised, confirm the current conditions in the official Visit Busan transport guide and the Busan bus information system’s transfer guide.

Are tourist passes worth it?

A tourist pass may be useful if you plan to visit several included attractions in a short period. It is not automatically the cheapest option. Before buying, list the attractions you genuinely intend to visit, check their current inclusion and opening hours, and compare the total with individual admission costs and transport expenses.

Read the conditions carefully: attraction admission, metro or bus rides, airport transfers and intercity transport may be treated differently. Never assume one pass covers every service.

Check the last train for your exact station and direction

Busan Metro does not have one citywide last-train time that works for every journey. The final departure varies by station, line and direction, and a transfer can require you to catch the last train on each leg. Before a late-night outing, open the official station timetable, select your boarding station and direction, and check the date-specific service information.

  • Check the last departure from the station where you will actually board—not just the time shown for a central station.
  • If changing lines, confirm the last train on the connecting line as well as the first train.
  • Leave a buffer for walking to the platform, finding the correct direction and crowded station entrances.
  • On event nights, check special service notices separately; normal timetables may not show temporary extensions or extra trains.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Assuming a tourist pass includes every bus, train or airport service.
  • Forgetting to tap off a bus when required.
  • Assuming WOWPASS spending balance and T-money balance are one shared balance.
  • Planning a late-night journey without checking the last service.
  • Relying on old fare screenshots or social posts instead of current official information.
  • Underestimating walking time between a station, bus stop and destination.

A quick checklist before leaving

  • Check your route, transfer points and last service for the return trip.
  • Make sure your transport card has enough balance and that you know how to top it up.
  • Save your destination in Korean as well as English when possible.
  • Allow extra time when changing between metro and bus.
  • Recheck current fares and rules close to your travel date.

A practical payment plan for overseas visitors

  • Metro and mixed journeys: Buy a T-money, Cashbee/EZL or another card accepted in Busan, then top it up with cash at a participating convenience-store counter or a station recharge machine.
  • Bus-only journey: If your overseas Alipay or WeChat Pay app is supported, scan the ZeroPay QR code displayed inside the city bus and show the completed payment screen to the driver.
  • Transfers: Use the same transit card and tap when boarding and getting off the bus. Busan's transfer rules generally require changing to another transport mode within 30 minutes; eligibility and exceptions depend on the route.

Do not assume that an overseas Apple Pay or Google Pay wallet can be tapped directly at every Busan gate. Mobile ticket products and card compatibility can differ. Keep a physical card or another payment option as a backup.

Useful Korean at a convenience store: “교통카드 충전해 주세요” (Please top up this transit card). Recharge amounts and card purchase prices can vary by shop and card type. Ask the cashier to confirm the amount before paying.
