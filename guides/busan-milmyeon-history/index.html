Busan Milmyeon: The History of Refugee-Era Noodles

A cold noodle dish shaped by displacement, scarce ingredients and the resourcefulness of people rebuilding their lives.

LOCAL FOOD HISTORY · Food origins can be disputed; this guide distinguishes documented context from stories that remain uncertain.

What is milmyeon?

Milmyeon is a Busan-style cold noodle dish made with wheat flour, often blended with starch, and served in chilled broth or with a spicy-sweet sauce. Recipes vary, but wheat-based noodles are its defining feature.

Why the Korean War matters

The Encyclopedia of Korean Culture describes milmyeon as a dish that emerged after 1950, when refugees from northern Korea came south. Buckwheat and familiar noodle ingredients could be difficult or expensive to obtain, while flour entered Busan through wartime relief supplies. Cooks adapted noodle-making traditions to the ingredients available. The story reflects displacement, shortages and the effort to recreate a taste of home.

More than one origin story

Naeho Naengmyeon in Uam-dong is often discussed because its family history reaches back to a noodle shop in Hamheung. Other accounts describe refugee families adapting northern-style cold noodles elsewhere in Busan. These stories matter, but they do not establish one uncontested inventor. It is more accurate to describe milmyeon as a food culture that took shape in postwar Busan.

How to order

Mul-milmyeon comes in cold broth; bibim-milmyeon is mixed with seasoned sauce. Many restaurants offer dumplings or sliced boiled pork alongside. Ask about spice level if you prefer mild food. Prices and opening hours change, so check current details before travelling.
