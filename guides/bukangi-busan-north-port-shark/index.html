Where Is Bukangi? The Busan North Port Shark Story

For weeks, a shark nicknamed Bukangi drew huge crowds to Busan North Port. It has not been reported in the area since October 27, raising a new question: has it moved out to open water?

UPDATED NOVEMBER 2, 20265 MIN READNOT A LIVE SIGHTING REPORT

Save this Busan Now story to Pinterest ↗

Before you go: Bukangi is not currently a reliable wildlife-viewing attraction. The latest reports available as of November 2 say it has not been observed since October 27. Visiting North Port does not mean you will see the shark.

What happened to Bukangi?

Bukangi is the nickname given to a shark first spotted on September 18, 2026, swimming in the artificial waterway at Busan North Port Waterfront Park in Dong-gu. The unexpected visitor became a local sensation, drawing residents, families, photographers and online creators to the waterfront.

After staying in the waterway for more than a month, the shark moved out to the sea during a guided movement operation on October 21. It was later seen in waters near North Port Pier 1 and, from October 24, near the International Passenger Terminal. It has not been reported in observations since October 27, according to a Hankyung report published November 1.

The latest confirmed situation

  • September 18: First spotted in the artificial waterway at North Port Waterfront Park.
  • October 21: Moved out of the waterway during a third attempt to guide it toward open water.
  • October 24–26: Reported in sea areas near the International Passenger Terminal.
  • Since October 27: No confirmed sightings have been reported in the latest coverage available as of November 2.
  • Around November 3: Authorities were expected to be in a better position to assess whether the shark had likely moved farther offshore. This is an expected assessment point, not confirmation that its location is known.

The available reports suggest that Bukangi may have moved farther offshore, but its route and current location have not been conclusively confirmed. A lack of sightings is not proof of a specific destination.

Why did Bukangi become such a big story?

At the height of the attention, the waterfront park drew as many as 100,000 visitors in a day, according to recent Korean news coverage. After the shark stopped appearing, reported visitor numbers fell sharply, with weekend and holiday attendance described as around 10,000 a day in the November 1 report.

The change shows how quickly a single unexpected wildlife encounter can turn a public waterfront into a destination—and how quickly visitor interest can shift when the animal is no longer visible.

Interest has also spread beyond the waterfront: Busan-based soju maker Daesun released a Bukangi-themed bottle. Read our Bukangi soju guide for what the special edition is and where visitors can ask for it. Retail stock is not guaranteed.

Can you still see Bukangi?

There is no reliable basis to promise a sighting. The shark has not been observed in the latest reports, and the sea areas where it was previously spotted include port waters that are not necessarily accessible to the public. Do not enter restricted areas, approach working docks, or rely on old social-media videos as evidence of a current sighting.

If you visit North Port Waterfront Park, treat it as a waterfront public space rather than a guaranteed shark-viewing spot. Enjoy the harbor scenery and nearby public areas, and check current official notices before making a special trip specifically for Bukangi.

What kind of shark is it?

News coverage has described Bukangi as a black shark, with the species identification reported by Korean authorities in October. This guide does not infer its current health, exact location or behavior from the lack of sightings. The most responsible interpretation is that its whereabouts remain uncertain.

Visitor information

North Port Waterfront Park is in Busan’s Dong-gu waterfront redevelopment area. If it is part of your itinerary, combine it with other accessible central Busan stops rather than planning an entire day around a possible sighting. Follow signs and staff instructions around the port, and keep clear of restricted operational areas.

  • Yonhap News, October 11, 2026 — earlier coverage of the crowds drawn to North Port by Bukangi.
