Jaecheopguk: The Lost Clam-Fishing Culture of Busan

A light clam soup preserves memories of an estuary livelihood that has largely disappeared from Busan.

LOCAL FOOD HISTORY · This guide separates documented history from local stories whose details remain uncertain.

What is jaecheopguk?

Jaecheopguk is a clear soup made with small freshwater clams known as jaecheop. It is associated with the Nakdong River estuary and the wider southeast of Korea, and is often remembered as a simple, refreshing meal or a morning soup.

The Nakdong estuary as a source of food

Busan’s Marine Natural History Museum has documented the history of local jaecheop fishing, soup vendors and restaurants through photographs, objects and oral histories. The estuary around today’s Samnak and Gupo was once known for clam harvesting. This was not only a food story: it was a livelihood involving tidal flats, harvesters, sellers and small eateries.

War, displacement and an affordable bowl

A Busan city story report notes that jaecheopguk was eaten by people who came to Busan during the Japanese colonial period and the Korean War, and describes how families stretched a purchased bowl with water and rice. That detail captures how inexpensive local ingredients could help households make a meal go further. The precise paths by which the dish spread varied, so the story is best understood as part of a wider culture of survival and local trade.

A tradition worth remembering

Local clam harvesting in the Busan estuary is no longer the everyday industry it once was. The city museum’s exhibition, “Jaecheop, Embracing Busan,” collected donated objects, photographs and memories from people who worked in clam fishing, vending and food businesses. The soup remains a way to remember the relationship between Busan residents and the river mouth, even as the old livelihood has faded.
