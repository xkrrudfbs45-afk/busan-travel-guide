Haeundae Neighborhood Guide: Beach, Food, Stays and Getting There

Haeundae is Busan’s best-known beach district. Use this page to plan the area in one place, then open the detailed guide for each decision.

Quick summary

  • Best for beach time, coastal walks, cafés and a relaxed seaside stay.
  • Haeundae Station is on Metro Line 2; from Busan Station, take Line 1 and change to Line 2 at Seomyeon.
  • The trade-off is distance from western and central Busan, so group days by area.

Is Haeundae the right base for you?

Choose Haeundae if the beach, coastal walks and evenings by the sea are a big part of your trip. It suits travelers who want an easy, resort-like base in the east of the city.

If most of your plans are in Nampo-dong, Jagalchi or the old downtown, compare Seomyeon as a more central base. Our where-to-stay guide compares both.

  • From Gimhae Airport: compare the light rail and Metro Line 2, a taxi and the airport limousine bus in our airport-to-Haeundae guide. It lists the details on the airport’s official page, checked November 10, 2026.
  • From Busan Station (KTX): take Metro Line 1 to Seomyeon and change to Line 2 toward Jangsan. Arriving late? Read the late-arrival guide first.
  • Getting around: a transit card covers the metro and buses. See the transit card guide.

Where to stay

Pick accommodation by its walking distance to the station or the beach, not just the word “Haeundae” in the hotel name. The district is long, and a hotel near Jangsan feels different from one by the beach.

Compare Haeundae with Seomyeon and Gwangalli in Where to Stay in Busan. For quieter coastal stays further east, see Songjeong and Gijang.

See and do nearby

  • Haeundae Blueline Park: popular rides can sell out, so check availability before you go.
  • Spa Land at Shinsegae Centum City: an indoor option for cold or rainy days.
  • Gwangalli, a few stops west on Line 2, and its Saturday drone show.
  • Night views and Haedong Yonggungsa Temple on the east coast.

Stay for the coast, but plan the return first

Haeundae is easy to enjoy at sunset, but the beach, Dongbaekseom and farther-east coastal stops do not all have the same walk back to a metro station. Before you set off, check the return leg from your final stop—not only the route that got you there.

  • Check the last metro from the station you will actually board and the direction you need. If you transfer, check the connecting line too.
  • For a coastal walk or a Sky Capsule visit, confirm the last practical bus or train from your finish point and allow time to reach the stop.
  • Use a taxi as a backup if the remaining walk is long, the route is steep, you are carrying luggage or the next service would make you miss a fixed-time plan. Compare the full journey and prepare your accommodation's Korean address.
  • Wind and rain can change how comfortable an exposed coastal walk feels. Check the day’s forecast and keep an indoor alternative.

Schedules, fares and service notices can change. Check the transit and last-train guide, the official Busan Metro timetable and the Busan Bus Information System for your date and route.

A simple Haeundae half day

  1. Morning: walk the beach and the coastal path around Dongbaekseom.
  2. Late morning: ride Blueline Park if you booked a slot.
  3. Lunch: Haeundae Market.
  4. Evening: Haeridan-gil cafés, or Gwangalli on a show night.

Add travel time between stops and keep one indoor backup for wind or rain.

Sky Capsule one-way: plan the return from Cheongsapo

The Sky Capsule runs one way between Mipo and Cheongsapo; your booking does not include a return ride. Compare a separately ticketed Haeundae Beach Train with the signed, publicly open Green Railway coastal walking route. Check the operator’s timetable, boarding station and remaining seats before choosing the train.

If walking, stay on the public pedestrian path and follow local signs and closures. Do not enter the railway track or fenced-off sections. Some coastal sections are exposed and include stairs; allow daylight and extra time, especially in wet or windy weather.

Marine City at night: skyline walk and a warm local meal

For a high-rise night view, add Marine City as a separate evening stop. The Korea Tourism Organization highlights the skyline view from Dongbaekseom Island and the Marine City coastal promenade; Bay 101 is a well-known waterfront viewpoint. From Dongbaek Station on Metro Line 2, check the walking route to the waterfront before you set out. The promenade is exposed to sea wind, so bring a layer and check weather conditions.

Centum City is a different stop. It is useful for shopping and indoor plans, while Marine City is the waterfront skyline area. Avoid treating them as one compact block: check the route and allow time to cross between them. If you want a warm meal after the walk, look for a currently open gukbap or haejangguk restaurant near your next stop; opening hours, break times and last orders vary by business, so confirm before walking over.

For an extended night walk, Busan's official YOLO Galmaetgil Route 4 connects Marine City, Cine Road, the yacht centre, Millak Waterside Park and Gwangalli Beach (about 5 km). It is a longer coastal route, not a quick stroll; check daylight, weather and your return transport before attempting the full section.

Common questions

Is Haeundae or Seomyeon better for a first trip?

Haeundae suits a beach-focused trip. Seomyeon is more central if your days are spread across the city. Many visitors stay in one and visit the other for a day.

How do I get from Busan Station to Haeundae?

Take Metro Line 1 to Seomyeon and change to Line 2 toward Jangsan, then get off at the station closest to your hotel.
