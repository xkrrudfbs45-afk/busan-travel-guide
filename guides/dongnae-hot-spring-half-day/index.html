A Half-Day in Dongnae: Hot Springs, History and Local Food

Dongnae offers a different side of Busan: a long-established hot-spring district, historic traces of the city and a neighborhood food scene that goes beyond the usual tourist checklist. Use this route as a flexible half-day plan.

NEIGHBORHOOD GUIDEHOT SPRINGS & CULTURECHECK DETAILS BEFORE VISITING

The short version: Make Hurshimchung the anchor of your visit, add Dongnae Eupseong if you want a history walk, and finish with a meal around Dongnae Market or Oncheonjang. You do not need to fit every stop into one afternoon.

The suggested route

  1. Hurshimchung: allow time to enjoy the hot-spring baths and facilities without rushing.
  2. Dongnae Eupseong: add a history-focused walk if you still have energy and the opening hours fit.
  3. Dongnae Market and dinner: browse the market area or choose a restaurant based on the food you feel like eating.

Open the three-stop route in Google Maps ↗. Walking conditions and travel time vary; check the route on the day and use a taxi or transit if the hills, weather or mobility needs make walking inconvenient.

1. Start with Hurshimchung (허심청)

Hurshimchung in Oncheonjang is a large traditional hot-spring bathing facility and one of Dongnae's best-known attractions. The Korea Tourism Organization describes more than 40 types of baths and capacity for up to 3,000 people. This is the choice for travellers whose main interest is a substantial hot-spring bathing experience.

  • Address: 32 Oncheonjang-ro 107beon-gil, Dongnae-gu, Busan
  • Hot-spring hours listed by VISITKOREA: 05:30–22:00; last admission 21:30
  • Jjimjilbang hours listed by VISITKOREA: 06:30–21:00
  • Admission listed by VISITKOREA: adults KRW 15,000 weekdays and KRW 18,000 weekends/public holidays. Confirm the current admission and any separate jjimjilbang charge before visiting.

Bathing areas are gender-separated and traditionally used without swimwear; shared sauna spaces have separate clothing rules. Follow the facility's current instructions, keep phones and cameras out of bathing and changing areas, and ask staff if anything is unclear.

2. Add Dongnae Eupseong for local history

Dongnae Eupseong is a historic fortress site associated with the defense of the area during the 1592 Japanese invasions of Korea. It offers a different pace from the hot-spring complex: a walk through the district's history and surviving or restored fortress features. The site includes slopes and outdoor paths, so wear comfortable shoes and check weather conditions.

VISITKOREA lists the site as generally open Tuesday to Sunday, 09:00–18:00, closed Mondays, with free admission. Hours and access can change for maintenance or events, so verify before making it a fixed stop.

3. Browse Dongnae Market

Dongnae Market is a useful place to see everyday neighborhood commerce and look for a casual meal or snack. It is not a single curated food hall: individual stalls and shops set their own hours, so the atmosphere and available food can vary by day and time. Treat the market as a flexible stop rather than relying on one specific stall being open.

4. Choose a meal that suits your appetite

Dongnae food is not limited to one dish or one style. If you want to connect the meal to local culinary tradition, look for Dongnae pajeon, the district's well-known scallion pancake. If you would rather have a different cuisine, the neighborhood also has notable Chinese and Thai options. These are alternatives for different tastes, not claims that every dish is a traditional Dongnae specialty.

For Chinese food: Mimiru (미미루)

Visit Busan describes Mimiru as a popular Chinese restaurant with a broader range of dishes than a typical Chinese-noodle shop. Its listing highlights jjajangmyeon and sweet-and-sour pork, and notes that queues can form at meal times. The published address is 91-1 Oncheonjang-ro, Dongnae-gu. Check the current menu, queue and opening details before travelling.

Mimiru map ↗ · Visit Busan restaurant listing ↗

For Thai food: Thai Bin (타이빈)

Thai Bin is listed by Visit Busan as a Thai restaurant recognized with the Thai SELECT Signature certification. The city listing highlights crab and soft-shell crab curry dishes. Its published address is 12 Geumgang-ro 73beon-gil, Dongnae-gu. Check current menus, prices, opening hours and booking requirements before you go.

For a local dish: Dongnae pajeon

Dongnae pajeon is a scallion pancake associated with this district and is a good choice if you want a meal with a stronger local connection. Recipes and restaurant styles vary. See our guide to the history of Dongnae pajeon before choosing a restaurant.

Which stops should you keep?

  • Mostly here for the hot springs: spend the bulk of your time at Hurshimchung, then choose one nearby meal.
  • Interested in history: add Dongnae Eupseong, but check its opening day and allow for outdoor walking.
  • Travelling with different food preferences: choose between Dongnae pajeon, Mimiru's Chinese dishes and Thai Bin's Thai menu rather than assuming everyone wants the same cuisine.
  • Short on time: skip the market or fortress rather than rushing through all three.

Practical planning notes

  • Dongnae and Oncheonjang are inland from Busan's main beach districts. Plan this as its own neighborhood visit rather than a quick detour between Haeundae and Nampo.
  • Check the latest opening hours and admission for Hurshimchung and Dongnae Eupseong before setting out.
  • Restaurant hours, menus, prices and queue arrangements change. Use the linked listings as starting points and confirm on the day.
  • For visitors with mobility needs, review the walking route and slopes around the fortress before committing to that stop.
