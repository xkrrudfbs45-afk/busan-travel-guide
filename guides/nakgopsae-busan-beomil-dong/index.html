Nakgopsae in Busan: Beomil-dong Origins & How to Order

A bubbling, spicy pot of octopus, beef intestines and shrimp is one of Busan’s signature comfort foods. Here is what the name means, how it connects to the old Jobang Nakji story, and what first-time visitors should know.

BUSAN FOOD5 MIN READINGREDIENTS & ORDERING TIPS

In one line: Nakgopsae (낙곱새) combines nakji (small octopus), gopchang (beef intestines) and saeu (shrimp), simmered in a spicy sauce and usually eaten over rice.

What does “nakgopsae” mean?

The name is a shorthand for its three main ingredients: octopus, intestines and shrimp. They cook together in a shallow pot with chili flakes, garlic, onion and green onion. The octopus is chewy, the intestines add richness, and the shrimp bring sweetness. The sauce becomes especially satisfying when spooned over rice.

Recipes vary by restaurant. The dish is usually spicy, and the stock or sauce may include seafood ingredients. It is not vegetarian or halal, and it is unsuitable for people with shellfish allergies unless the restaurant can clearly confirm a safe alternative.

Why is Beomil-dong connected to the story?

Busan’s nakji-bokkeum story is often linked to “Jobang Nakji.” “Jobang” refers to Joseon Bangjik, a textile mill that once operated in the Beomil-dong area. A restaurant near the mill became known for spicy stir-fried octopus, a filling dish workers could eat with a meal or drinks. Over time, related combinations such as naksae (octopus and shrimp), nakgop (octopus and intestines) and nakgopsae became familiar menu names.

The history is best understood as a neighborhood food tradition rather than a dish with one clearly documented inventor. The original restaurant is no longer operating, and restaurants serving related dishes can now be found in several parts of Busan.

For a current official tourism listing in the Beomil-dong area, see Oryukdo Nakji Beomil Main Branch on Visit Busan. Check its current menu, hours and closing days before making a special trip.

How to order it

  • 낙곱새 2인분 주세요. “Nakgopsae for two, please.”
  • 덜 맵게 해 주세요. “Please make it less spicy.” Ask before cooking starts; not every restaurant can adjust the spice level.
  • 새우 빼 주세요. “Please leave out the shrimp.” This is a request, not a guarantee against cross-contact or seafood in the sauce.

Many restaurants cook the pot at the table. Follow the staff’s instructions, wait until the ingredients are fully cooked, and start with a little sauce over rice before mixing in more.

A few things to know before you go

  • Plan for spice. Rice helps balance the sauce, but “less spicy” may still be hot.
  • Check the serving size. Some restaurants set a two-person minimum or price the dish per person.
  • Ask about allergens. Octopus, shrimp and anchovy-based stock may be involved; tell staff about allergies clearly.
  • Choose the area around your itinerary. Beomil-dong is associated with the dish’s history, but you do not have to cross the city if another reliable restaurant fits your plans better.
