Jagalchi Market: How to Avoid Surprise Bills

You do not need to avoid Busan’s famous seafood market. Just agree on the seafood, the weight and every extra charge before anything is prepared.

The most important rule: Before agreeing to buy, confirm the seafood species, quantity or weight, price, preparation method, per-person table setting fee and any extra charges. “How much is it?” is not enough if the final meal includes several separate fees.

1. Understand the market-to-table system

Jagalchi is one of Busan’s best-known seafood markets. Inside the main market and nearby seafood markets, you can browse live and dried seafood; some vendors also connect customers with restaurants where seafood is sliced, steamed, grilled or made into stew. The market building and surrounding stalls are not all the same business, so prices, services and hours can differ.

At places using the market-to-table model, the seafood purchase and the meal service may be charged separately. A restaurant may charge a per-person table-setting fee and additional preparation or cooking fees. These charges vary by shop—do not assume one market-wide rate.

2. Ask these questions before choosing seafood

  • What species is it? Ask the seller to name the seafood and point to the exact fish or shellfish you are buying.
  • How much are you buying? Confirm whether the price is per kilogram, per fish, per plate or for a set.
  • What is the final seafood price? Ask for the charged weight and total cost before the seller prepares it.
  • How will it be prepared? Raw fish, steaming, grilling and spicy fish stew may have different preparation charges.
  • What will the restaurant charge? Confirm the table-setting fee per person, cooking fees, rice, stew, side dishes and drinks.
  • Can you show me the total? Ask for the amount in writing, on a display or on an order slip before you agree.

Take a moment to compare a few stalls before deciding. You are free to browse first; there is no need to accept the first offer. Be polite, clear and calm rather than treating every vendor as untrustworthy.

3. Check the weighing process without making assumptions

Seafood is often sold by weight, so make sure you understand what is being weighed and what weight is being charged. Watch the weighing and ask whether the container or basket weight has been excluded. If you are unsure, ask the vendor to explain the displayed weight and price before the seafood is cut or cooked.

Do not assume that every difference in weight means wrongdoing. The goal is to understand the calculation before you commit. If the quoted amount changes, pause and ask for the new total before proceeding.

4. Separate the seafood price from meal fees

Use this checklist before moving upstairs or handing over the seafood:

  • Seafood: species, quantity or charged weight, unit price and subtotal.
  • Preparation: slicing, steaming, grilling or other cooking fees.
  • Table setting: per-person charge, if applicable.
  • Extras: rice, spicy fish stew, drinks and additional dishes.
  • Payment: whether you pay the seafood seller and the restaurant separately, and which payment methods each accepts.

A low seafood price does not automatically mean the whole meal will be cheaper. Compare the final total for the meal you actually want.

5. Korean phrases to use

이 생선은 1kg에 얼마예요?
How much is this fish per kilogram?

몇 그램으로 계산하나요?
What weight are you charging me for?

바구니 무게는 제외했나요?
Has the basket weight been excluded?

손질비와 상차림비가 각각 얼마예요?
How much are the preparation fee and table-setting fee, separately?

추가 비용을 모두 포함하면 총 얼마예요?
What is the total including all additional charges?

주문하기 전에 총액을 보여주세요.
Please show me the total before I order.

6. When to pause or walk away

  • You still do not know the species, quantity or total price after asking.
  • The quoted price changes and nobody explains why.
  • You are being asked to accept extra dishes or services you did not request.
  • The seller cannot clearly explain preparation and table charges.
  • You feel rushed and have not agreed to the total.

Simply say thank you and move on if the terms are unclear. If you have already paid and believe the amount differs from what was agreed, keep the receipt or order slip and ask the business to review the charge. For serious disputes, note the stall or shop name and the details while they are fresh.

7. Choose seafood by season—but check what is available

Seafood availability and prices change with season, weather and daily supply. Rather than arriving with a fixed species in mind, ask what is in season and request the price for the portion you want. Autumn is often associated with gizzard shad and prawns, while winter is popular for yellowtail; actual availability and prices vary. If you are not familiar with the species, ask the vendor to write its name or show you the price board.

8. Getting there and planning your visit

The main market is at 52 Jagalchihaean-ro, Jung-gu, Busan. The official Visit Busan listing says it is about a six-minute walk from Nampo Station Exit 2 or Jagalchi Station Exit 10 on Metro Line 1. Listed hours are 5:00 a.m.–10:00 p.m., with regular closing days shown as the first, third and fifth Tuesdays; schedules can differ across the surrounding market and individual businesses, so check before travelling.

After your meal, you can continue to BIFF Square, Gukje Market or Bupyeong Kkangtong Market. Keep the route flexible if the market is busy.

Remember: The best protection against an unexpected bill is a clear agreement before the seafood is weighed, prepared or served. Ask about the full total, compare options, and only proceed when you understand what you are paying for.
