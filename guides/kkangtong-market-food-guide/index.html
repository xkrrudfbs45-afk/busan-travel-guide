What to Eat at Busan’s Kkangtong Market

Come for the market atmosphere, stay for the snacks. One distinctive stop is Kkang Dwaehu, known for its crispy fried pork galbi. Here is how to find it and make the most of the surrounding market streets.

Start here: If you want one memorable dish, look for Kkang Dwaehu (깡돼후), a market food stop built around bite-sized pork galbi that is coated and fried. Then browse the surrounding alleys for other snacks rather than treating the market as a single restaurant destination.

1. Kkang Dwaehu: Busan-style fried pork galbi

Kkang Dwaehu (깡돼후) is known for dwaeji-galbi twigim (돼지갈비튀김): pork rib pieces fried until crisp, served as a shareable snack. It is a playful twist on familiar Korean pork galbi, with a crunchy outside and a rich, savory center. The shop is inside the Bupyeong Kkangtong Market area, making it an easy food stop while exploring Nampo-dong.

  • What to order: fried pork galbi; some listings also describe seasoned and half-and-half versions. Menu availability can change.
  • Useful Korean name: 깡돼후 (Kkang Dwaehu).
  • Phone listed by public directories: +82-51-244-2019.
  • Finding the shop: search the Korean name in your map app. Public listings show differing street-number details within the market area, so use the shop name and phone rather than relying on one copied address.
  • Hours: public listings vary and note that the shop may close early when sold out. Check the current listing or call before making a special trip.

2. Keep exploring: classic market snacks

Bupyeong Kkangtong Market is not just one famous shop. Visit Busan highlights snacks and market foods such as tteokbokki, multteok (rice cakes served in broth), fried tofu pockets and old-fashioned fried chicken. The stalls and individual shops do not all keep the same hours, so browse what is open and choose a few small portions to share.

  • Tteokbokki: chewy rice cakes in a spicy-sweet sauce.
  • Multteok: fish-cake broth with rice cakes, a warming street snack.
  • Yubu pockets: fried tofu pockets filled with glass noodles and vegetables.
  • Busan eomuk: fish cake, often served hot on skewers with broth.

More local food stops around Nampo-dong

The market is a good starting point, but some of Nampo-dong’s best-known meals are just outside the market lanes. These are separate stops rather than stalls inside the night market.

Kkangtong Golmok Halmae Yubu Jeongol (깡통골목할매유부전골 본점)

For a warm, comforting bowl, look for the main store of Kkangtong Golmok Halmae Yubu Jeongol at 29 Bupyeong 3-gil, Jung-gu. Public listings describe its signature yubu-jeongol (fried tofu pockets in broth), yubu-bottari udon and bibim dangmyeon. It is a market-style meal rather than a fried street snack.

  • Address: 29 Bupyeong 3-gil, Jung-gu, Busan.
  • Phone listed: +82-51-245-1878.
  • Listed hours: around 10:00–18:00; confirm before visiting because small market shops can change schedules.

Nampo Jjukkumi (남포쭈꾸미)

Local recommendation: Go2Busan’s Busan-based contributor recommends Nampo Jjukkumi for its moderately spicy seasoning — flavorful heat without being overwhelmingly hot. The restaurant serves seasoned jjukkumi (small octopus), with options that public listings describe alongside thin-sliced beef or pork. Diners can check the available spice level when ordering.

  • Address: 4 Gwangbok-ro 18beon-gil, Jung-gu, Busan.
  • Phone: +82-51-245-0492 (listed in local directories).
  • Listed hours: roughly 11:30–23:00; Sunday listings may close earlier. Confirm on the day.
  • Tip: ask for the mild or medium option if you prefer a gentler level of heat.

No. 18 Wandang House (18번완당집)

Wandang is a Busan-style wonton soup with very thin wrappers and a light broth. The well-known 18번완당집 near BIFF Square is at 31 BIFF gwangjang-ro, Jung-gu, making it an easy stop while exploring Nampo-dong. It is a different experience from spicy market food: gentle, warm and soup-focused.

  • Address: 31 BIFF gwangjang-ro, Jung-gu, Busan.
  • Phone: +82-51-245-0018.
  • Listed hours: around 10:30–20:00, with a short afternoon break shown by some listings. Check before you go.
  • Useful Korean name: 18번완당집 (남포동).

Find 18번완당집 in Nampo-dong on Google Maps ↗

About the Seomyeon name: Public listings also show a restaurant called 18번완당 in Seomyeon, at 16 Seomyeonmunhwa-ro. It is a separate listing from the 18번완당집 at BIFF Square. Do not confuse the Seomyeon shop with the Nampo-dong restaurant or describe it as the Nampo branch’s main store.

3. Visit after dark for the night-market atmosphere

The market operates during the day, but the evening brings a different energy. Visit Busan lists the night market from 19:30 to midnight, while the Korea Tourism Organization lists 19:30–23:30. Because schedules can vary by season and market section, treat these as a guide and check locally on the day.

Expect narrow aisles, food carts, small tables and a lively crowd. Arrive hungry, but avoid ordering too much at the first stall: it is more fun to sample a few things as you walk.

4. How to get there

  • Market address: 48 Bupyeong 1-gil, Jung-gu, Busan.
  • By subway: Jagalchi Station on Busan Metro Line 1, Exit 3, followed by roughly a 9-minute walk according to Visit Busan.
  • By bus: the tourism listing names Bupyeong Market as a nearby stop; check your route from your hotel.
  • Market hours: general shops are commonly listed around 08:00–20:00, but individual shop hours vary. Night-market stalls are listed in the evening.

Make it a Nampo-dong market walk

Bupyeong Kkangtong Market sits beside Gukje Market, and Jagalchi Market is also within the wider old-downtown area. A practical route is to browse Gukje Market during the day, visit Bupyeong Kkangtong Market for food, then continue toward Jagalchi if you want to see the seafood market. You do not need to cover every market in one visit; choose the stops that match your interests.
