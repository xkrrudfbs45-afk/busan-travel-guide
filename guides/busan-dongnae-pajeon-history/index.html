Dongnae Pajeon: A Scallion Pancake with Local Roots

A soft, seafood-rich pancake brings together ingredients from Busan’s coast and nearby fields—but some origin tales remain folklore.

GO2BUSAN LOCAL GUIDELOCAL FOOD HISTORYHISTORY & PRACTICAL TIPS

LOCAL FOOD HISTORY · This guide separates documented history from local stories whose details remain uncertain.

Not an ordinary crispy pancake

Dongnae pajeon is a thick scallion pancake associated with Dongnae in Busan. It commonly combines long green onions with seafood and a rice-flour batter, then cooks covered so the inside stays soft and moist. It is often served with a tangy dipping sauce rather than treated like a thin, crisp jeon.

Mountain fields, coastal seafood and the market

Busan city features and Korean government cultural writing connect the dish with Gijang scallions, known for their strong aroma and mild sweetness, and seafood from the nearby coast. The opening of the Donghae Nambu railway in the 1930s helped Gijang vendors bring produce to Dongnae, strengthening the relationship between local ingredients and the market town. Dongnae’s market culture helped the dish become widely known.

Royal tribute stories: handle with care

A popular story says a Dongnae official presented the pancake to the king on the third day of the third lunar month. Other accounts connect it to banquet food served by the local gisaeng training institution, later sold in the market. These stories are part of local memory, but reliable evidence for a single definitive origin is limited. It is safer to describe them as traditions and focus on the documented relationship between ingredients, trade and local food culture.

How to enjoy it

The texture is softer and more substantial than many thin pancakes. Let it cool slightly before cutting, and try it with the house dipping sauce. Spring is often associated with good scallions, but restaurants serve the dish beyond that season. Check current prices and opening hours before visiting.
