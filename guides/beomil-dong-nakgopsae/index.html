LOCAL FOOD · BEOMIL-DONG

Beomil-dong Nakgopsae: The Story Behind Busan’s Spicy Hot Pot

A bubbling pan of octopus, beef tripe and shrimp is one of Busan’s most distinctive comfort foods. Here’s what nakgopsae is, how to eat it and how to plan a food stop around Beomil-dong.

What is nakgopsae?

Nakgopsae combines nakji (small octopus), gopchang (beef tripe) and saeu (shrimp) in a spicy, savoury sauce. The ingredients simmer together at the table, creating a rich sauce that is usually eaten with rice. As the pan cooks down, the sauce becomes more concentrated, so stir occasionally and follow the restaurant’s guidance.

Why the district is called Jobang

“Jobang” is a shortened form of Joseon Textile Company (Joseon Bangjik), a large cotton-textile factory established with Japanese capital in Beomil-dong in November 1917. It began operating in 1922 and became one of the major mechanised textile plants of the colonial period. The name survived the factory itself: the area around today’s Beomil-dong, Jayu Market and Pyeonghwa Market is still commonly associated with “Jobang” or “Jobang-ap,” meaning the area in front of the factory.

A brief history of the factory district

  • 1917: Joseon Textile Company was established in Beomil-dong under Japanese capital.
  • 1922: The factory began full operations, processing cotton through spinning and weaving.
  • 1930: Korean women workers took part in a major labour dispute. Contemporary newspaper records describe conflict over demands including wage increases and working hours. The district’s history is therefore not only industrial growth, but also colonial-era labour exploitation and resistance.
  • 1968–1969: The factory closed and the company was dissolved; the large site was redeveloped. The place-name “Jobang” remained in everyday Busan speech.

That history gives “Jobang nakji” a deeper local context. Busan’s official cultural storytelling describes the spicy octopus dish as a local food tradition associated with the former factory neighbourhood and its workers. However, detailed origin stories differ, so it is more accurate to present the dish as a neighbourhood tradition than to claim one proven inventor or a single definitive recipe.

Where Gaemijip fits in

Gaemijip is a well-known name in Busan’s nakgopsae story, but the restaurant’s history should not be confused with the origin of the word “Jobang.” Busan tourism material identifies the well-known Gaemijip main branch with Nampo-dong’s Gukje Market and dates its opening to 1972. Beomil-dong is important because of the old Joseon Textile Company and the local “Jobang nakji” food tradition; that does not by itself mean Gaemijip began at the factory site. Because restaurant names and branches can be confusing, check the exact branch and address before visiting.

How locals eat it

Start with a little sauce over rice, then add more as you get used to the heat. Some diners mix the cooked ingredients and sauce directly into rice; others order fried rice if the restaurant offers it. Ask for less spicy seasoning if needed, and check whether tripe is included in the version you order.

Plan your visit

Search for “조방낙지” or “낙곱새” around Beomil Station and confirm the exact branch, current menu and opening hours before travelling. Restaurants can differ in spice level, portion size and whether they use octopus alone or the full nakgopsae combination. Use a map search rather than relying on an old address.

A local tip

Nakgopsae is a hot, filling meal best enjoyed without a rushed schedule. If you are travelling with someone who avoids shellfish or offal, check every ingredient with staff before ordering. The dish is not automatically suitable for people with seafood allergies.

Useful Korean phrases for ordering

These short phrases help confirm spice level, serving size and extra charges before the pan is cooked.

  • 낙곱새 2인분 주세요. — Nakgopsae for two, please.
  • 덜 맵게 해 주세요. — Please make it less spicy.
  • 곱창 빼 주세요. — Please leave out the beef intestine. Ask whether this variation is available.
  • 공깃밥은 포함인가요? — Is rice included?
  • 볶음밥 추가하면 얼마예요? — How much extra is fried rice?
  • 총 얼마인가요? — What is the total?

Before you order

  • Check current opening hours, last order and holiday closures.
  • Ask about minimum portions and whether rice, noodles or fried rice cost extra.
  • Confirm spice level and ingredients, especially offal, shellfish and seafood if you have dietary restrictions.
  • Use the exact branch address when opening a map; similar restaurant names can lead to the wrong location.
