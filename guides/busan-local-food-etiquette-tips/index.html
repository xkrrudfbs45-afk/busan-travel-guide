The Local’s Guide to Busan Food: Pork Soup & Seafood Etiquette

You do not need to know every rule to eat well in Busan. Start with a few useful phrases, season your soup gradually and ask clear questions before ordering seafood.

Updated November 20265-minute readBy Go2Busan, a Busan-based guide
TL;DR — order with confidence
  • Dwaeji gukbap is usually served with seasonings and condiments on the side; add a little at a time and taste as you go.
  • Solo dining is possible at many soup shops, but policies and busy-time seating vary by restaurant.
  • At seafood markets, confirm the total price and any preparation or table-setting charges before ordering.
  • For Gwangalli, check the current drone-show schedule and weather rather than assuming it runs every night.

1. How to season dwaeji gukbap

Dwaeji gukbap (돼지국밥) is pork-and-rice soup and one of Busan’s signature comfort foods. Depending on the restaurant, the broth may arrive mild, with chopped scallions, salted shrimp, seasoning paste, chili, pepper or chives served separately. There is no single mandatory way to season it.

Local approach: Taste the broth first. Add a small amount of salted shrimp or seasoning, stir, then taste again. Some diners add rice to the soup; others eat the rice separately. Both approaches are common. If you are sensitive to salt or spice, add less first—you can usually add more, but you cannot easily remove it.

Useful phrases

새우젓은 따로 주세요. — Please serve the salted shrimp separately.

덜 맵게 해 주세요. — Please make it less spicy.

밥은 따로 주세요. — Please serve the rice separately.

2. Eating alone without awkwardness

Soup restaurants, noodle shops and many casual eateries can be convenient for solo travelers, but seating rules differ. Some places may have minimum orders, shared tables or limited seating for one during the busiest meal periods. Do not assume that every restaurant accepts solo diners at all times.

Local approach: Visit slightly before or after the peak lunch rush when your schedule allows, look for a menu with individual portions and ask before sitting down. If a restaurant cannot accommodate one person, treat it as a venue policy rather than a personal rejection.

Show this Korean order card

혼자 식사할 수 있나요?

“Can I eat here alone?”

돼지국밥 하나 주세요.

“One pork soup, please.”

포장도 되나요?

“Is takeout available?”

3. Jagalchi seafood: ask about the whole bill

Jagalchi Market is a landmark for seafood, but the experience differs between market stalls, restaurants inside the market and nearby eateries around Nampo-dong and Jungang-dong. Some venues price seafood separately from preparation or table service; others use set menus or clear per-person pricing.

Local approach: Before you order, confirm the seafood price, portion or weight, preparation fee, side dishes, drinks and any per-person charge. Ask whether the quote is the total for your group. If the answer is unclear, choose a place with a menu and prices you can understand. A posted price is more useful than a vague promise that something is “fresh.”

Price-check phrase

조리비와 상차림 비용까지 포함한 총액이 얼마인가요?

“What is the total, including cooking and table-setting charges?”

메뉴판을 볼 수 있을까요?

“May I see the menu?”

4. Make Gwangalli dinner fit the evening

Gwangalli combines a beach promenade, bridge views, cafés and seafood restaurants. It can be especially busy on pleasant evenings and event nights. The Busan drone show has a published schedule that may change for weather, special events or operational reasons; do not plan dinner around an assumed showtime.

Local approach: Check the Visit Busan official tourism site and current local notices for the latest event information. If watching a show is your priority, allow time to arrive, find a viewing spot and leave afterward. If dinner matters more, reserve where possible and enjoy the waterfront before or after the busiest period.

5. Match the food to the neighborhood

  • Seomyeon: A central choice for casual restaurants, bars and easy onward connections.
  • Nampo-dong and Jagalchi: Useful for market browsing, seafood and a walk through the older downtown area.
  • Gwangalli: A waterfront evening for bridge views, cafés and seafood restaurants.
  • Haeundae: A coastal base for beach time and nearby attractions; popular places can be busy at peak times.

These are broad starting points, not guarantees of quality or price. Check the menu, recent operating information and route for the specific restaurant you choose.
