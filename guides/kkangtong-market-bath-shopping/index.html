Where to Buy Korean Bath & Sauna Essentials in Busan

A different kind of Busan souvenir: the bath and sauna tools Koreans use at home and in jjimjilbangs. Around Bupyeong Kkangtong Market, small specialist shops sell exfoliating towels, soaps, bath salts and massage accessories.

Why visit? If you are curious about Korean bathhouse culture, this is a chance to browse practical items rather than generic souvenirs. Recent local reporting in October 2026 highlighted specialist bath-product shops inside the Kkangtong Market area, including Yeongdo Halmeoni (영도할머니).

Korean bathhouse culture: mogyoktang, jjimjilbang and seshin

A mogyoktang (목욕탕) is a Korean public bathhouse focused on washing and soaking. A jjimjilbang (찜질방) usually adds heated rooms and shared rest areas, and many facilities combine both. They are related experiences, but the terms are not interchangeable.

For generations, neighborhood bathhouses were more than places to wash: they were familiar community spaces, especially when private bathrooms were less common. Home bathrooms are now widespread and bathhouse habits have changed, but public baths and jjimjilbangs remain a distinctive part of everyday Korean leisure.

Seshin (세신) is professional body exfoliation. The familiar Italy towel (이태리타월) is a textured Korean exfoliating cloth; its name does not mean it was made in Italy. If you try one, soak first, use gentle pressure and stop if your skin stings or becomes irritated. A rough scrub is not suitable for every skin type.

1. Yeongdo Halmeoni (영도할머니)

Yeongdo Halmeoni is a small bath-product shop reported at 28 Bupyeong 3-gil, Jung-gu, Busan. Recent visitor reports describe a focused selection of Korean bath and sauna items. The shop is not a spa or public bathhouse; it is a place to buy the products used for bathing and body care.

2. What to look for

  • Korean exfoliating towels (이태리타월 / 때타월): textured washcloths used to scrub away dead skin after soaking. They come in different textures and shapes; choose a gentler option if you have sensitive skin.
  • Bath soaps and body cleansers (때비누): soaps marketed for body exfoliation or a deep-clean feeling. Check the ingredients and intended use.
  • Bath salts (입욕 소금 / 목욕 소금): products added to bathwater. Check the label for ingredients, directions and whether the product is suitable for your skin.
  • Body scrubs: exfoliating products in different textures and scents. Avoid using abrasive scrubs on irritated or broken skin.
  • Gua sha and massage tools (괄사 / 마사지 도구): small tools for massage and body-care routines. Ask the seller to explain the material and intended use.
  • Sauna accessories: small practical items such as hair caps and folding cushions may also appear among the stalls.

3. Practical shopping tips

  • Bring a little cash: recent visitor accounts describe cash as useful at small specialist shops. Card and transfer policies can vary by store; ask before paying.
  • Check the label: look for product ingredients, directions, manufacturer and country of origin. Ask for help if the label is only in Korean.
  • Ask before photographing: these are small working shops. Be considerate of staff and other customers.
  • Do not assume every item is a bargain: compare sizes and prices, and check whether a set is cheaper than buying items individually.
  • Pack carefully: keep bath salts, soaps and liquids sealed in a separate pouch for the journey home. Check airline liquid limits for carry-on luggage.

4. Where is the shopping area?

The bath-product shops are in and around Bupyeong Kkangtong Market in Jung-gu, close to Gukje Market and the Nampo-dong shopping district. Use the Korean shop name and address when navigating; small shop entrances can be easy to miss in the market lanes.

  • Market address: 48 Bupyeong 1-gil, Jung-gu, Busan.
  • Yeongdo Halmeoni address reported: 28 Bupyeong 3-gil, Jung-gu, Busan.
  • Subway: Jagalchi Station, Busan Metro Line 1; walk toward Bupyeong Kkangtong Market.
  • Market hours: general market hours are commonly listed as 08:00–20:00, but individual shops set their own schedules.

Make a half-day Nampo-dong outing

Combine a little shopping with the neighborhood’s food and market culture. Browse Gukje Market, look for bath and sauna goods around Bupyeong Kkangtong Market, then stop for fried pork galbi or other market snacks. If you have more time, continue toward Jagalchi Market.
