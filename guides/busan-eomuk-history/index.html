Busan Eomuk: Fish Cake, Markets and Port-City History

A familiar street snack carries a history of migration, industry and postwar food-making in Korea’s port city.

What is Busan eomuk?

Eomuk is made by seasoning minced fish and shaping the paste before steaming, frying or grilling it. “Busan eomuk” refers to fish cake produced in Busan, and its modern identity grew alongside the city’s markets and seafood trade.

Colonial-era shops and Japanese influence

The Academy of Korean Studies notes that Korea had fish-based dishes before modern fish-cake factories, while the modern Busan product was influenced by Japanese kamaboko. During Japanese colonial rule, Japanese-run fish-cake shops operated around Bupyeong Market in central Busan. This history should not be simplified into an unchanged ancient custom: the modern industry developed through colonial-era exchange and unequal power relations.

After liberation and the Korean War

After liberation, Korean-owned production expanded. During the Korean War, many refugees gathered in Busan, and the city’s fish supply supported affordable processed foods. The Encyclopedia of Korean Culture describes how Busan eomuk became more popular during this period. In the 1950s, producers around Bongnae Market on Yeongdo and Yeongju-dong helped the industry grow; other manufacturers followed in later decades.

From market snack to local symbol

Eomuk is easy to eat while walking through a traditional market, but taste depends on fish blend, starch, seasoning and cooking method. Ask what a set includes. If you have a seafood allergy, do not assume a fish-cake snack is safe because it looks different from other seafood.
