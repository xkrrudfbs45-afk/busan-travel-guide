At a glance: published and planned winter dates

  • Seomyeon Light Festival: October 29, 2026–February 14, 2027, around Seomyeon 1st Street; daily lighting hours are not confirmed in the current notice.
  • 3rd Millak Luce Festa: November 14, 2026–January 10, 2027, at Millak Waterside Park; daily 18:00–23:30, free admission.
  • Christmas Village Busan: November 26–December 25, 2026, at Busan Cinema Center; paid, timed entry on 21 selected days.
  • 13th Haeundae Light Festival: November 28, 2026–January 10, 2027, around Haeundae Beach, Gunam-ro and Haeundae Square; 18:00–23:00, free admission.
  • Gwangbok-ro Winter Light Tree Festival: Jung-gu’s September 8 re-notice specifies a planned lighting period of December 4, 2026–January 31, 2027, 17:30–22:30. This is the lighting-installation schedule, not confirmation of every performance or visitor programme.

Update checked November 25, 2026: Busan City's official event calendar confirms Haeundae Light Festival lighting hours of 18:00–23:00 and free admission. For December 5, Busan City's November 24 Daily Busan article lists the Street Festa from 16:00–22:00 and the Balcony Music Shows on December 12 and 19 from 18:00–21:00. A separate English event-calendar listing still gives 16:00–20:00 for the Street Festa, so check the organiser's latest post before planning around the final hour. Haeundae Light Festival: a beach-and-downtown evening

The city calendar lists the 13th Haeundae Light Festival from November 28, 2026, to January 10, 2027. Its listed area covers Haeundae Beach, Gunam-ro and Haeundae Square. This makes it easiest to pair with a Haeundae-area dinner, beach walk or nearby stay rather than treating it as a stop on a Nampo-dong evening.

Allow time to walk between the beach and Gunam-ro, and check the district’s latest notice for lighting hours, performances, access arrangements and any weather-related changes before setting out.

Haeundae lighting hours: the official Busan event listing and the Ministry of Culture, Sports and Tourism list 18:00–23:00. The lighting ceremony is scheduled for November 28 at 18:00 on Haeundae Beach. Admission is free. A separate VisitKorea page currently shows a conflicting date range; use the newer Busan City and Ministry listings for this edition and recheck the organizer before travel.

Opening-night program (Nov 28 only): The official Visit Busan schedule lists the ceremony from 18:00 to 19:00 on the sand in front of the event plaza at Haeundae Beach. The opening choir is scheduled for 18:00–18:15, fireworks for 18:15–18:25 and the Serenade main performance for 18:25–18:45. These timings are for opening night only, not a daily programme. Check the detailed Visit Busan schedule (Korean) (opens in a new tab) for updates before you go.

Easy local route: Start at Haeundae Station (Metro Line 2, Exits 3 or 5), walk along Gunam-ro towards Haeundae Beach, then follow the illuminated beachfront area. The district’s access guide lists this station route; check the last-train time for your own destination rather than assuming the festival’s 23:00 lights-off time is the last safe departure. 3rd Millak Luce Festa: another waterfront light walk

The 3rd Millak Luce Festa runs from November 14, 2026 to January 10, 2027 at Millak Waterside Park. Busan City lists daily lighting hours of 18:00–23:30 and free admission. It is a separate light-up event from the Gwangalli M Drone Light Show, so check each venue and programme separately rather than assuming the drone show is part of the festival.

Use the park as a separate evening stop or pair it with a meal around Millak and Gwangalli. Check the organizer’s latest notice for weather-related changes. Busan City — 3rd Millak Luce Festa (opens in a new tab) · Millak Waterside Park map (opens in a new tab).

Gwangalli M Drone Light Show: a Saturday night option

During October–February, the free show is scheduled every Saturday at 19:00 and 21:00. Busan City describes each performance as lasting about 12 minutes. The official overview says the first and third weeks of each month are Drone x Laser Shows, while the second and fourth weeks are regular drone-light shows. The November 2026 programme lists November 28: “Avengers: Doomsday in Gwangalli”. The official December programme notice (opens in a new tab) lists December 5 “Secrets of the Deep”, December 12 “Frozen Kingdom: The Lost Sun”, December 19 “Anpanman’s Winter Vacation in Gwangalli”, December 26 “Santa & Friends: Mission Complete!” and a “2027 Countdown” on Thursday, December 31. That notice does not give the December 31 showtime, so check the organiser’s special notice before planning New Year’s Eve around it. Check the Busan City schedule and cancellation notice (opens in a new tab) and the official show website (opens in a new tab) before leaving.

Pairing it with Haeundae: the 19:00 show may leave time for a later visit to the Haeundae Light Festival, which stays lit until 23:00, but the two venues are in different districts. The show itself lasts about 12 minutes; reaching the next venue also requires the walk from the beach, a transit transfer and another walk. Check the live route and the last train for your hotel before attempting both. Do not treat the 21:00 show as a reliable starting point for a full Haeundae visit.

Weather, strong winds, typhoons or technical/communication issues can delay or cancel a show, sometimes on the day. Check the official notice before leaving your hotel. See the full Gwangalli drone-show viewing guide.

Gwangbok-ro Street Festa: December 5, 2026

The Korean-language Visit Busan event notice (opens in a new tab) lists the Street Festa on December 5, 2026, 16:00–22:00, and Balcony Music Shows on December 12 and 19, 18:00–21:00. However, the English-language Busan City event listing (opens in a new tab) gives a different Street Festa end time of 20:00 and shows an older November Balcony Music Show schedule. Because the official listings conflict, treat the Street Festa closing time as not fully reconciled and check the organizer's latest notice at @starrynightbusan (opens in a new tab) before fixing your evening. Jung-gu’s separate winter-light installation schedule is planned for December 4, 2026–January 31, 2027, 17:30–22:30; it is not the event programme.

December 5 is also a Gwangalli show night: the official December programme lists “Secrets of the Deep” (winter-season shows at 19:00 and 21:00), subject to weather cancellation. The two venues are in different districts and the transfer is tight after the Street Festa ends; do not promise that both can be comfortably attended. Check the live route and allow extra time.

Christmas Village Busan: a ticketed-event check matters

Christmas Village Busan runs from November 26 to December 25, 2026 at Busan Cinema Center, but it is open on 21 selected days, not every day: every Thursday–Sunday from November 26 through December 25, plus December 21–23 (Monday–Wednesday). The official YES24 notice lists hours of 16:00–22:00 on weekdays and 11:00–22:00 on weekends and December 24–25.

Standard prices are ₩5,900 for weekday and weekend daytime entry, ₩7,900 for weekend evening entry, and ₩14,900 for the Christmas-season ticket on December 24–25. Children under 36 months are admitted free with proof of age. Discounts, remaining inventory and ticket conditions can change, so check the live sales page before paying: YES24 — Christmas Village Busan 2026 tickets (opens in a new tab) · official ticket-opening notice (opens in a new tab). See our full Christmas Village guide for the operating-day calendar and international-visitor booking cautions.

Its location in Centum City makes it practical to combine with an indoor shopping or dining plan. If you also want the Haeundae Light Festival, both are on Metro Line 2, but they are not adjacent stations; allow time for the ride, walking and your ticketed entry window rather than assuming the two venues are next door.

Gwangbok-ro winter lights: planned dates, not the full programme

Busan Jung-gu’s September 8, 2026 re-notice for lighting installation and event operations specifies a planned lighting period of December 4, 2026–January 31, 2027, 17:30–22:30. The listed area covers Gwangbok-ro, Gwangbokjungang-ro and hillside-road sections in Bosu-dong, Daecheong-dong and Yeongju-dong. This is a procurement and operating schedule, not a complete visitor programme or confirmation of a lighting ceremony, performances, admission arrangements or parking. The notice allows for changes, so check the district’s latest public announcement before travelling. Open the Jung-gu official re-notice (opens in a new tab).

For a shorter old-town evening, combine the Gwangbok-ro section with BIFF Square, Gukje Market or Bupyeong Kkangtong Market. The Sanbok-ro sections are separate hillside neighborhoods rather than one continuous flat walk from Nampo; choose the section that fits your time and mobility. Check each market’s closing time and do not assume you can comfortably add Haeundae to the same short evening.

Seomyeon Light Festival: a central-city evening walk

The 2026 Seomyeon Light Festival runs from October 29, 2026 to February 14, 2027 around Seomyeon 1st Street. Busanjin-gu’s opening notice lists an 18:30 lighting ceremony on October 29; a district report says the display covers about 1.9 km across five themed zones. The current material we checked does not confirm daily lighting hours or every final installation point, so do not reuse last year’s 18:00–23:00 schedule. Start near Seomyeon Station (Lines 1 and 2), then keep the rest of the evening around Seomyeon or nearby Jeonpo rather than trying to combine it with Haeundae on a short night. Read the Busanjin-gu event report (Korean) (opens in a new tab).

Which winter event should you choose?

  • For a beach atmosphere: choose Haeundae and build the evening around the beach, Gunam-ro and nearby restaurants.
  • For a venue-based Christmas outing: check the Christmas Village ticket and program details at Busan Cinema Center.
  • For old-town shopping and street food: use the planned Gwangbok-ro lighting window as a guide, then check the final district programme and keep Nampo-dong and Haeundae on separate evenings.

Practical winter planning

Coastal wind can make an evening feel colder than the daytime forecast suggests. Wear layers, bring a wind-resistant outer layer and comfortable shoes, and keep an indoor alternative in mind. Crowds can affect walking speed and public transport access; follow local staff instructions and check transit notices after major events.
