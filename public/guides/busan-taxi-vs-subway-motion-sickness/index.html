Busan Taxi or Subway? Choose the Right Ride for Your Route

Taxis can help with luggage or awkward transfers, while the metro is often easier to predict in traffic. Choose by route and comfort, not blanket assumptions.

BUSAN · GETTING AROUND5 MIN READPRACTICAL LOCAL TIPS

Page last updated: Nov 23, 2026

Quick answer: For many cross-city trips, the subway is predictable and avoids road traffic. A taxi may be useful for luggage, late-night transfers or places far from a station. If winding roads make you motion-sick, compare the exact route rather than assuming every taxi ride will feel the same.

1. When the subway is a good choice

  • Travelling between major areas connected by metro.
  • Moving during rush hour when road traffic can be slow.
  • Keeping costs predictable for a solo traveller or small group.
  • Reducing the amount of time spent on winding hillside roads.

Check station exits and walking distance at both ends. A route with several transfers or many stairs may be less convenient with heavy luggage.

2. When a taxi can make sense

  • Travelling with bulky luggage or several people.
  • Reaching a destination far from a metro station.
  • Making a short transfer where the saved walking time is worth the fare.
  • Travelling at a time when public transport is limited.

Traffic and road conditions vary. Use the destination name and exact map pin, confirm the route in your map app, and check the fare estimate where available. Do not assume a taxi can drive directly to a pedestrian-only attraction entrance.

3. Taxi apps for visitors: what to check before you ride

k.ride, Kakao Mobility’s service for international visitors, lists English, Japanese, Traditional and Simplified Chinese support. Its official guide says destination search and driver chat support 133 languages. The service supports overseas-issued cards, but sign-up may still ask for a mobile number or additional account/device verification. A Korean number is not guaranteed to be unnecessary: install the app, test your own number and add a payment method before the day you need a ride.

k.ride documents automatic payment through the registered payment method after the ride. Do not assume you can switch every booking to cash paid directly to the driver. Korea Tourism Organization’s transport guide also lists foreign-issued cards as accepted by taxi-hailing services including k.ride and Uber Taxi, and by taxi card readers. Chinese visitors using DiDi should note that the Busan DiDi integration is a separate arrangement; it is not a universal app option for every nationality.

Save your hotel name and full street address in Korean, pin the exact entrance, and check the pickup point before confirming. If app registration or payment fails, use an official taxi stand or another transport option rather than relying on an unverified payment workaround.

Busan has hills, coastal roads and winding approaches to some viewpoints. If those roads trigger symptoms for you, consider a subway route for the longest part of the journey and use a short taxi transfer only for the final stretch. On a road journey, looking ahead toward the direction of travel and avoiding reading while moving may help some people.

Busan taxi fares: check the meter, not a verbal quote

Standard medium taxi · checked 17 November 2026: Busan City and the Busan Taxi Transportation Association list the fare adjusted on 1 June 2023 as the current standard fare.

Fare itemStandard medium taxi
Base fare₩4,800 for the first 2 km
Distance fare₩100 per 132 m
Time fare₩100 per 33 seconds below 15 km/h
Late-night surcharge23:00–04:00: 20%; 00:00–02:00: 30%
Out-of-area surcharge30% outside Busan's taxi service area
Night + out-of-area combined surcharge40% from 23:00–00:00 and 02:00–04:00; 50% from 00:00–02:00

Deluxe, large and tourist taxis use different fare systems. The final meter fare also depends on distance, traffic, waiting time and applicable surcharges.

Before the taxi moves: check that the meter is running. A standard taxi should charge the metered fare; an unexplained fixed “tourist price” is not the normal fare. Busan City lists charging above the meter, an unexplained detour and starting the meter before the passenger boards among examples of improper fare collection.

If the fare looks wrong: keep the receipt, app trip record, vehicle number, date, time and pickup/drop-off points. Photograph the meter only if it is safe. Do not argue if you feel unsafe; move to a safe public place first.

For multilingual tourist help and complaints, call 1330 in Korea or +82-2-1330 from overseas, or use the Korea Tourism Organization Tourist Complaint Center (opens in a new tab). 1330 can provide tourist interpretation and route complaints to the relevant department; it is not an emergency service.

4. Luggage and station access

Check elevator access at the exact station exits you plan to use. If you arrive before hotel check-in, compare station lockers, staffed luggage storage and luggage delivery services. Confirm opening hours, cut-off times, delivery coverage and baggage rules directly before relying on a service.

5. Compare the whole trip

Do not compare only the vehicle time. Include walking to the stop, waiting, transfers, stairs, traffic, luggage and the return trip. For two or more travellers, compare the total taxi fare with the combined transit fares rather than comparing one taxi fare with one transit ticket.

Before you go

  • Check the full door-to-door route.
  • Confirm last-train or bus times if travelling late.
  • Save your destination in Korean as well as English when possible.
  • Consider hills, luggage and motion sensitivity.
