Do You Need Cash in Busan? Market, Street Food & Card Guide

South Korea is highly card-friendly, but a little cash still makes market snacks and small street purchases easier. Here is how to prepare without exchanging more money than you need.

Is Busan a cashless city?

Cards are widely used in Busan. You can normally pay by card at department stores, supermarkets, chain cafés, hotels, larger restaurants and many attractions. Contactless and mobile payment options vary by card issuer and terminal, so keep your physical card available.

The catch is the small transaction: a market snack stall, an independent street-food cart or a tiny shop may prefer cash, have a minimum card amount, or use a terminal that does not accept your overseas card. This is not a rule for every stall. Payment methods differ from seller to seller, even in the same market.

Where cards are usually convenient

  • Department stores and shopping malls: cards are standard at established retailers.
  • Hotels and larger restaurants: international cards are commonly accepted, but check before ordering at independent venues.
  • Convenience stores and chain cafés: card payment is generally straightforward.
  • Metro and buses: use a compatible Korean transit card or a supported mobile payment option; do not assume an ordinary bank card can be tapped directly at every gate.

When carrying cash helps

Keep a small amount of Korean won for traditional-market snacks, street-food carts, small independent vendors, coin lockers or unexpected small purchases. At places such as Jagalchi and Bupyeong Kkangtong Market, ask the individual seller whether your card is accepted before you order. A market is not one single payment system: one shop may accept cards while the next prefers cash.

For seafood at Jagalchi, payment method is only one part of the conversation. Confirm the seafood, weight, unit price, preparation charge, table-setting fee and extras before the food is prepared. Do not assume that every stall refuses cards or charges visitors differently.

How to get cash with a travel card

  1. Look for an ATM displaying your card network, such as Visa, Mastercard, Plus or Cirrus. ATMs in convenience stores, bank branches and transport hubs may offer international-card access, but not every machine supports every card.
  2. Check your travel-card app for overseas ATM withdrawal settings, limits and fees before you try.
  3. Choose Korean won (KRW) if the ATM offers to charge you in KRW or your home currency. If it offers a conversion rate, compare it with your card issuer's rate; dynamic currency conversion is often less favourable.
  4. Review the ATM's own fee and your card issuer's fee before confirming. Keep the receipt and take your card and cash.

Practical tip: You do not need to carry a large amount. Start with a modest cash buffer, then withdraw more only if your plans involve markets, small stalls or places that clearly require it.

Useful Korean phrases

  • 카드 결제돼요? — Can I pay by card?
  • 현금만 가능한가요? — Is cash the only option?
  • 가장 가까운 ATM이 어디예요? — Where is the nearest ATM?
  • 수수료가 얼마예요? — How much is the fee?

Before you head out

Carry one physical payment card, keep a little Korean won for small purchases, and check your travel card's ATM settings. For market meals, confirm the total before ordering. That is usually enough to avoid the most common payment surprises without carrying a thick wallet.
