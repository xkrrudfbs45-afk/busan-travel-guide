Seoul to Busan KTX: Booking, Sold-Out Tickets & Waitlist Tips

A practical guide to booking the train to Busan—and what to try when every seat looks sold out, including waitlists, cancellations and alternative departures.

2026 rail integration update: Since September 1, 2026, Korea’s high-speed rail services have operated under the integrated KTX system. Former SRT routes, including services from Suseo, are now sold as KTX through Korail’s official booking site (opens in a new tab) or the Korail+ app. SR’s official Q&A says SRT tickets were sold only for trains running through August 31; from September 1, former SRT routes are booked as KTX through Korail+ or Korail’s official website. The old SRT app and website’s limited non-member access was a temporary transition exception, not a separate booking route for current journeys. Search by your exact travel date and departure station; do not look for a separate SRT booking channel. SR’s official integration Q&A (Korean) (opens in a new tab).

Quick answer: Start with Korail’s official English booking site (opens in a new tab). For foreign rail travelers, Korail officially expanded the advance booking window from one month to two months on October 1, 2026. The usual opening time is 07:00 Korea Standard Time. Some Korail English booking and FAQ screens may still show the older one-month wording, so check the live booking form for your exact date and follow any special holiday notice. Confirm availability on the official site.

For most visitors, the official Korail website is the clearest place to check train times, departure stations, ticket conditions and the total fare. Use the official result as your reference even if you later compare another booking platform.

Pay with an overseas card and check the ticket record

  1. Use Korail’s multilingual website and search for your exact departure station, arrival station, travel date and passenger count.
  2. Read the live payment screen and the latest notices before paying. Korail’s official 2023 notice confirms overseas-issued credit cards are supported on its foreign-language booking website; it does not guarantee that every card or station vending machine will work.
  3. If checkout asks you to enter an email address and password, save them exactly as entered. Use the current MY TICKET screen to check the booking and open the actual ticket. Confirm that the ticket is issued before travelling; a payment attempt or receipt alone is not proof of a valid boarding ticket.

If payment fails: check MY TICKET and your card or bank transaction status before submitting another payment. If no ticket record appears, check Korail’s latest notice, confirm the first attempt is not completed, then retry only if appropriate or ask a station ticket office / official customer channel about your next step. Avoid duplicate payment attempts.

2. When do KTX tickets go on sale?

For foreign rail travelers, Korail announced that the advance booking window was expanded from one month to two months before departure from October 1, 2026, with the usual opening time at 07:00 KST. Some English booking and FAQ screens may still display the older one-month wording. If the live booking form for your date shows a different window, follow the current form and check Korail’s latest notice. Special holiday periods can use separate sale schedules. Read Korail’s October 8, 2026 announcement ↗ (opens in a new tab).

If your date is not available yet, check the official notice and return when the booking window opens. If the train you want is sold out, search nearby departure times and read the actual options shown by Korail rather than assuming every train on the route is full.

When every KTX seat looks sold out

  1. Check the Korail+ waitlist option. Korail says waitlist requests for sold-out trains can be made online until two days before departure when the option is shown. Canceled seats are assigned in application order. A waitlist request is not a confirmed ticket; check the booking details and pay by the deadline displayed if a seat is assigned.
  2. Search a wider time range. Check earlier and later trains, not only the exact departure you first wanted. Compare the full journey and the station shown on each result.
  3. Ask at a station ticket office about a standing ticket. Korail’s FAQ says standing tickets may be available for sold-out trains at station counters. Availability and conditions vary; never board without a valid ticket.
  4. Check again for returned seats—but do not chase a supposed reset hour. Korail does not publish a universal nightly cancellation-seat release time. Cancellations can appear when other passengers change or refund tickets, so there is no reliable basis for promising that seats reset at 2 a.m. or 3 a.m. A Korean KTX-community post dated September 19, 2026 (opens in a new tab) described a seat appearing at about 3:20 a.m.; that is one person's report, not a schedule. Based on a recent firsthand booking check, Go2Busan's editor also recommends trying again around 5:00 a.m. Korea time if you are still looking. Both are anecdotal checks, not official release times or guarantees. Do not wait until 5:00 a.m. to start: use the waitlist and notifications where available, and check again at intervals.

Do not rely on screenshots, unofficial resale posts or a payment attempt as proof of a ticket. Confirm that the final ticket appears in your Korail booking record before travelling.

Before paying: verify the travel date, number of passengers, departure and arrival stations, train time, passenger names where requested, and the final fare. Keep the confirmation details until your journey is complete.

3. Check the departure station carefully

Do not plan your trip around the phrase “Seoul to Busan” alone. Your ticket identifies the actual departure station and train. Read the station name on the confirmed booking, then check how long it takes to reach that station from your hotel or airport connection.

Most Seoul–Busan itineraries are associated with Seoul Station, but services and departure points can vary. Use the station printed on your ticket, not an assumption based on the destination.

4. What to know before boarding

  • Keep a valid ticket ready. Open the ticket in the official service or follow the issuing platform’s exact instructions. A booking receipt or voucher may not be the same as the boarding ticket.
  • Check the train number and seat. Match the train number, departure time, car and seat shown on your ticket before boarding.
  • Arrive with a buffer. Give yourself time to navigate the station, find the platform information and handle luggage. Large stations can be busy.
  • Follow station displays. Platform and boarding information can change; use the live station boards and announcements.

5. KTX luggage space: where does a large suitcase go?

KORAIL’s passenger transport terms allow each passenger to carry up to two items, provided they do not occupy a seat or block the aisle. The terms put the responsibility for looking after those items on the passenger. They do not set a simple KTX suitcase-size limit in centimetres, so do not confuse KTX with separate commuter-rail or airline baggage rules. You must be able to move your own luggage safely and stow it without obstructing other passengers.

Where to put your suitcase

  • Large suitcase: Look for the designated luggage rack around the vestibule between cars. The exact layout and free space can vary by train and carriage; it is not a reserved space, so do not assume a spot will be available.
  • Small cabin bag: Use the overhead rack only if the bag fits securely and can be lifted without risk. Do not force a heavy suitcase onto a high shelf.
  • At your seat: Keep the floor area clear for your feet and other passengers. Never leave a case in the aisle, doorway or emergency access area, or use an empty seat as storage.

Seat and boarding strategy

If the live seat-selection map shows a luggage icon or storage-area symbol, use it as a clue when comparing seats—but check the actual train and car, because layouts differ. If no symbol is shown, do not guess based on a seat number. Arrive at the platform with a little extra time, check the car number on the platform display, and board with enough time to find a suitable rack without rushing. Early boarding improves your chance of choosing from the available space; it does not reserve a rack.

What if you have an extra-large case?

Before travel, measure the case including wheels and handles and make sure you can lift or roll it safely. Keep passports, medication, electronics and valuables with you. If your case is too heavy to handle, you are travelling with several large bags, or you cannot find safe space, arrange luggage delivery or staffed storage instead of blocking the aisle.

Important distinction: The often-quoted 32 kg / 158 cm limits belong to separate commuter-rail rules, not the KTX passenger-transport clause. KTX’s published rule focuses on two items and keeping seats and passageways clear. See KORAIL Passenger Transport Terms, Article 22 (Korean) (opens in a new tab).

Need storage after arrival? Busan City lists a staffed Zimcarry counter inside KTX Busan Station near Exit 5 on the first floor, plus lockers at Metro Line 1–4 stations. See our Busan Station luggage storage and delivery guide before you travel.

6. If payment does not work

If an overseas card is declined, re-check the card details and any verification prompt, then follow the payment provider’s instructions. You can compare the official site with another booking channel, but review the final fare and change/refund conditions before paying twice. Confirm whether the first attempt actually failed before making a second booking.

Arriving at Busan Station late at night: what to do next

If your KTX reaches Busan late, do not assume the subway or your connecting bus will still be running. The time printed on your train ticket is not the time you will reach your hotel: allow for getting off the train, finding the exit, collecting luggage and walking to the next stop.

1. Check the entire route—not just the first train

  • Search from Busan Station to your hotel’s exact address, not just “Seomyeon” or another broad district.
  • Check the last departure for every leg, including any transfer from metro to bus. A connection can become impossible even when the first service is still running.
  • Recheck the route on the day of travel because schedules, service notices and journey times can change.

2. If public transport has finished

Use the official taxi queue at the station or a reputable taxi-hailing service. Before the ride, show the driver your hotel name and Korean address, confirm the destination on the app or map, and check the fare estimate where available. Keep the meter or app fare visible. Do not accept an unsolicited offer from someone approaching you inside the station.

Taxi fares can vary with distance, traffic, vehicle type and applicable late-night surcharges. This guide does not publish a fixed Busan Station–Seomyeon or Busan Station–Haeundae fare because a single estimate can mislead travelers.

3. Make the hotel part of the plan

  • Confirm that the front desk or self-check-in process will be available at your expected arrival time.
  • Save the hotel’s Korean address and phone number offline.
  • If you have heavy luggage or are travelling with children, compare the full door-to-door taxi cost with the public-transport route before departure.

Important: There is no single “last train time” that works for every visitor. Your destination, weekday, transfer and walking time all matter. Check the official Busan Transportation Corporation (opens in a new tab) information and the route planner for your exact date before boarding your KTX.

7. After you arrive at Busan Station

Decide how you will continue from the station before departure day. If your accommodation is not ready, you may want to compare luggage storage or delivery options. Storage means you return to the same location to collect your bags; delivery is a separate service with its own booking conditions.
