Rail-transfer reality check: Compare the full door-to-door journey, not just the train ride. Include the walk from the terminal to the light-rail station, transfer corridors and lifts, waiting time, and the final walk from the metro station to your hotel. With a large suitcase, a child or limited mobility, allow more time for transfers and check elevator access before choosing the route. Quick answer: For a budget-friendly journey, take the Busan-Gimhae Light Rail Transit to Sasang and transfer to Busan Metro Line 2 toward Haeundae/Jangsan. A taxi is simpler with heavy luggage, several travelers or a late arrival. If you are considering Airport Limousine 1, check the branch: Busan City lists airport departures to Jangsan until 16:50 and to Gijang until 21:40. Option 1: Light rail + Metro Line 2

Follow airport signs to the Busan-Gimhae Light Rail Transit station. Ride to Sasang, then transfer to Busan Metro Line 2 in the direction of Haeundae and Jangsan. Get off at the station that best matches your accommodation; Haeundae Station is not the closest stop for every hotel in the wider district.

This is usually the first option to compare if you are traveling on a budget and your arrival leaves enough time for the connection. Allow extra time for finding the station, buying or topping up a transport card, changing platforms and walking from the final station to your hotel.

For a broader explanation of cards and transfers, read our Busan public transportation guide.

Option 2: Taxi

A taxi can be more convenient if you have several bags, are traveling with children or a group, or would rather avoid a transfer after a long flight. The actual journey time and fare depend on traffic, the precise destination and any applicable surcharges. Use the official taxi queue, confirm the hotel address, and check the fare method before the trip begins.

If you are comparing a taxi with a pre-booked vehicle, check the pickup point, waiting-time policy, flight-delay procedure, luggage capacity, cancellation terms and total price—not just the headline fare.

Option 3: Airport or city bus

Airport Limousine 1 serves the Haeundae–Gijang corridor; Line 2 serves Seomyeon–Bujeon. These are separate services. Check the exact stop, direction and current timetable before boarding. City buses are a different service and have different luggage rules.

Do not choose a bus solely because it appears direct. Compare the walk from the stop to your hotel, the expected traffic and the time of your arrival.

Travelling with a large suitcase? Check the city-bus limit. Visit Busan says ordinary city buses generally allow hand luggage under 20 kg per person and under 50 × 40 × 20 cm (about a 20-inch carry-on). Larger cases or bulky bags should use the subway, airport limousine or taxi.

Do not rely on the former Route 307 exception. Busan’s trial on city bus 307, which ran between Gimhae Airport, Centum City, Haeundae and Songjeong, allowed one suitcase up to 30 inches per passenger at any time, but the published trial period was 1 July–30 September 2026. As of 22 November 2026, no later official extension notice was found. A Busan Ilbo test ride on 26 August (opens in a new tab) reported about 90 minutes from Haeundae to the airport and no dedicated suitcase restraint on Route 307; that was one dated test, not a current timetable. City bus 307 is separate from Airport Limousine 1; the two services have different stop patterns and timetables.

Official sources: Visit Busan transport guidance (opens in a new tab) · Busan City’s Route 307 luggage-trial notice (opens in a new tab).

Airport limousine buses are operating. Official fare and timetable details checked 22 November 2026. The two routes serve different corridors; they are not one bus covering every stop.

  • Line 1 — Haeundae / Gijang: adult fare KRW 9,500. Busan City lists separate first/last departures from the airport: Jangsan-bound 07:10–16:50 and Gijang-bound 10:10–21:40. Jangsan services run about every 20–60 minutes; Gijang services are less frequent. Busan City’s 5 January 2026 update adds an East Busan Tourism Complex stop: it is labelled Osiria Theme Park on airport-bound trips and Matie Osiria on Gijang-bound trips; Dongam Back Gate (Ananti Cove) is also on the Gijang branch. The current airport route text does not show the Osiria stop, so confirm the stop with the operator or a live map for your direction. Not every departure continues to Gijang.
  • Line 2 — Seomyeon / Bujeon: adult fare KRW 6,000 from 5 January 2026, reduced from KRW 7,500. Airport departures are listed 07:20–21:30; Busan City gives a 60–80 minute interval.
  • Boarding and payment: both services use Platform 3, first floor. Cash is not accepted; the airport currently lists credit-card-only payment. Busan City also lists Bustago QR booking and ticket machines near Gate 4; card boarding may be possible if seats remain. No free transfer. Busan City lists up to two bags per passenger, total weight below 30 kg; confirm the operator's current rule.

Official sources: Busan City’s 5 January 2026 fare and service update (opens in a new tab) · Gimhae Airport’s current bus information (opens in a new tab) · Bustago (opens in a new tab).

Before leaving the airport

  • Save your hotel’s full address and nearest station or bus stop.
  • Check the last rail departure for your travel date; landing time is not the same as the time you reach the platform.
  • Keep a backup option if your flight is delayed or baggage collection takes longer than expected.
  • Check whether your hotel has late check-in instructions.
  • Use a route planner for the final journey and recheck it before leaving the terminal.

Routes, timetables, fares and airport bus operations can change. Verify the latest details with Visit Busan’s official transportation guide (opens in a new tab) and the relevant operator before departure.

Common questions

What is the budget-friendly way to reach Haeundae?

The light rail and metro combination is often a budget-friendly option, but it involves transfers. Check current fares, service hours and the route to your exact accommodation before travelling.

Is a taxi easier with luggage?

A taxi can reduce transfers and walking with heavy luggage, but the fare is usually higher and road traffic affects travel time. Compare the convenience against your budget and arrival time.

What if my flight arrives late?

Check the last light rail, metro and bus services for your arrival date rather than relying on a general timetable. If public transport will not work for your arrival, compare a taxi with a pre-booked transfer.
