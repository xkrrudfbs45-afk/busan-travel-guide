Jagalchi Market: How to Avoid Surprise Bills

You do not need to avoid Busan’s famous seafood market. Just agree on the seafood, the weight and every extra charge before anything is prepared.

1. Understand the market-to-table system

Jagalchi is one of Busan’s best-known seafood markets. Inside the main market and nearby seafood markets, you can browse live and dried seafood; some vendors also connect customers with restaurants where seafood is sliced, steamed, grilled or made into stew. The market building and surrounding stalls are not all the same business, so prices, services and hours can differ.

At places using the market-to-table model, the seafood purchase and the meal service may be charged separately. A restaurant may charge a per-person table-setting fee and additional preparation or cooking fees. These charges vary by shop—do not assume one market-wide rate.

2. Ask these questions before choosing seafood

  • What species is it? Ask the seller to name the seafood and point to the exact fish or shellfish you are buying.
  • How much are you buying? Confirm whether the price is per kilogram, per fish, per plate or for a set.
  • What is the final seafood price? Ask for the charged weight and total cost before the seller prepares it.
  • How will it be prepared? Raw fish, steaming, grilling and spicy fish stew may have different preparation charges.
  • What will the restaurant charge? Confirm the table-setting fee per person, cooking fees, rice, stew, side dishes and drinks.
  • Can you show me the total? Ask for the amount in writing, on a display or on an order slip before you agree.

Take a moment to compare a few stalls before deciding. You are free to browse first; there is no need to accept the first offer. Be polite, clear and calm rather than treating every vendor as untrustworthy.

Ask for the full total before the seafood leaves the tank

The most useful moment to clarify the bill is before the seafood is weighed, cut or sent upstairs. Confirm the species, charged weight, preparation method, per-person table fee and extras together. This protects both sides from misunderstandings without assuming that every vendor uses the same pricing model.

3. Check the weighing process without making assumptions

Seafood is often sold by weight, so make sure you understand what is being weighed and what weight is being charged. Watch the weighing and ask whether the container or basket weight has been excluded. If you are unsure, ask the vendor to explain the displayed weight and price before the seafood is cut or cooked.

Do not assume that every difference in weight means wrongdoing. The goal is to understand the calculation before you commit. If the quoted amount changes, pause and ask for the new total before proceeding.

4. Separate the seafood price from meal fees

Use this checklist before moving upstairs or handing over the seafood:

  • Seafood: species, quantity or charged weight, unit price and subtotal.
  • Preparation: slicing, steaming, grilling or other cooking fees.
  • Table setting: per-person charge, if applicable.
  • Extras: rice, spicy fish stew, drinks and additional dishes.
  • Payment: whether you pay the seafood seller and the restaurant separately, and which payment methods each accepts.

A low seafood price does not automatically mean the whole meal will be cheaper. Compare the final total for the meal you actually want.

A documented price-display case: In September 2025, news reports described Jung-gu taking administrative action after a Jagalchi-area restaurant displayed a 70,000-won seafood dish without a clearly displayed price. This was a reported case involving one business—not evidence that every market stall has the same problem. Use it as a reminder to check the displayed price and full bill before ordering. 5. Korean phrases to use

이 생선은 1kg에 얼마예요?
How much is this fish per kilogram?

몇 그램으로 계산하나요?
What weight are you charging me for?

바구니 무게는 제외했나요?
Has the basket weight been excluded?

손질비와 상차림비가 각각 얼마예요?
How much are the preparation fee and table-setting fee, separately?

추가 비용을 모두 포함하면 총 얼마예요?
What is the total including all additional charges?

주문하기 전에 총액을 보여주세요.
Please show me the total before I order.

영수증 주세요.
Please give me a receipt.

6. When to pause or walk away

  • You still do not know the species, quantity or total price after asking.
  • The quoted price changes and nobody explains why.
  • You are being asked to accept extra dishes or services you did not request.
  • The seller cannot clearly explain preparation and table charges.
  • You feel rushed and have not agreed to the total.

Simply say thank you and move on if the terms are unclear. If you have already paid and believe the amount differs from what was agreed, keep the receipt or order slip and ask the business to review the charge. For serious disputes, note the stall or shop name and the details while they are fresh.

7. Choose seafood by season—but check what is available

Seafood availability and prices change with season, weather and daily supply. Rather than arriving with a fixed species in mind, ask what is in season and request the price for the portion you want. Autumn is often associated with gizzard shad and prawns, while winter is popular for yellowtail; actual availability and prices vary. If you are not familiar with the species, ask the vendor to write its name or show you the price board.

8. Getting there and planning your visit

The main market building is at 52 Jagalchihaean-ro, Jung-gu, Busan. Visit Busan and the Korea Tourism Organization both list 05:00–22:00. KTO lists the first and third Tuesdays as closing days; Visit Busan also lists the fifth Tuesday. Because the official closing-day lists differ, check the market's current notice before planning a visit; individual stalls and restaurants may keep different schedules.

The Korea Tourism Organization lists the market phone number as +82-51-245-2594. The main building is about a six-minute walk from Nampo Station Exit 2 or Jagalchi Station Exit 10 on Metro Line 1, according to Visit Busan.

9. Staying near Gwangalli? Compare Millak Hoe Town

Jagalchi is the natural choice for Nampo-dong, BIFF Square and the old-town market area. If you are staying near Gwangalli or want seafood with a view of Gwangan Bridge, compare Millak Hoe Town in Suyeong-gu. Visit Busan describes a first-floor live-fish market with restaurants on the upper floors. Its official listing gives the complex address as 1 Millaksubyeon-ro, Suyeong-gu, phone +82-51-757-3000, and daily hours of 09:00–02:00; individual shop hours and service fees differ. Ask the restaurant for its current per-person table charge and whether preparation or maeuntang is extra before buying fish.

  • Getting there: about 18 minutes on foot from Gwangan Station Exit 3 on Metro Line 2. Bus 210 stops at Jinro Beach Apartment; buses 38, 41, 83, 83-1 and 108 stop at Millak Golmok Market, followed by a 7-minute walk. Parking is paid.
  • Access: Visit Busan lists a ramp, elevator, wheelchair-accessible facilities and an accessible restroom.
  • Do not confuse the buildings: Millak Hoe Town is at 1 Millaksubyeon-ro. The separate Millak Hoe Center (Millak Fishermen's Live Fish Market) is at 60 Gwanganhaebyeon-ro 312beon-gil, phone +82-51-753-0200; VisitKorea lists it as open year-round with parking.
  • Winter fish: Visit Busan's food guide names yellowtail (bangeo, 방어) as a winter seasonal fish. Availability and price change with the day's supply. Ask for today's price per kilogram and confirm the weight before preparation; do not treat an online price from another market as a Busan quote.
  • Park rule: Millak Waterside Park has been an alcohol-free zone since 1 July 2023. If you want a drink with your seafood, eat at a restaurant rather than planning to drink in the park.

Quick questions

Jagalchi or Millak — which should I choose?

Choose Jagalchi for Nampo-dong and Metro Line 1. Choose Millak if you are staying near Gwangalli and want restaurants facing Gwangan Bridge. At both places, ask about the shop's current service and table fees before ordering.

When is bangeo in season?

Visit Busan identifies yellowtail as a winter seasonal fish. The catch and price vary, so ask for the price and weight on the day.

Can I drink at Millak Waterside Park?

No. Suyeong-gu's official report records the designation notice on 23 May 2023 and its effective date as 1 July 2023. Read the Suyeong-gu official implementation report (PDF) (opens in a new tab).

Are Millak Hoe Town and Millak Hoe Center the same building?

No. Millak Hoe Town is at 1 Millaksubyeon-ro; Millak Hoe Center is at 60 Gwanganhaebyeon-ro 312beon-gil. Check the address before setting out.

What is a chojangjib?

At Jagalchi Market, the seafood market and the dining room can be separate steps. Choose seafood from a first-floor stall, confirm the price and how it is calculated, then ask how to reach a second-floor chojangjib (a restaurant that prepares and serves the seafood you purchased).

The dining venue may charge a per-person table or setting fee. Side dishes, vegetables and dipping sauces may be included, while cooking, steaming, rice, drinks and maeuntang (spicy fish stew) can cost extra. The exact arrangement and price vary by restaurant and seafood order.

Ask before you go upstairs

  • “Is there a table-setting fee per person?”
  • “Does the fee include side dishes and vegetables?”
  • “How much are preparation and maeuntang?”
  • “Is rice or a cooking fee charged separately?”

Do not rely on an old per-person price quoted online. Ask the stall and the restaurant to confirm every charge before the fish is prepared. If you are not sure, pause the order and ask for the total in writing or on a calculator.

Busan City's visitor guide describes the first-floor purchase and second-floor chojangjib arrangement at Jagalchi, including separate charges for dining and additional dishes. Read Busan City's Jagalchi Market food guide (opens in a new tab).

Ask about payment before the seafood is prepared

Do not assume every shop has the same card-payment policy, and do not generalize individual complaints to the whole market. Before choosing seafood, ask whether the shop accepts your card and confirm the full amount, including seafood, preparation, table setting and extras.

Useful Korean: 카드 결제 가능한가요? (Can I pay by card?) · 이게 전부 얼마인가요? (How much is everything in total?) · 추가 비용이 있나요? (Are there extra charges?) If the price is by weight, confirm the weight and unit price before preparation.

For broader payment planning, see Do You Need Cash in Busan? Market, Street Food & Card Payment Guide. Card acceptance varies by individual seller; carry a little cash as a backup rather than assuming the whole market follows one rule.
