# Go2Busan SEO Content Standard

This checklist applies to every new or substantially refreshed Go2Busan article in English, Japanese, and Taiwan Traditional Chinese.

## 1. Search intent before drafting

- Identify one primary search intent and one clear reader task. Examples: compare transport options, choose a neighborhood, avoid an extra fee, plan a visit, or find a specific local food.
- Use Search Console query/page data first when the page already exists. For a new topic, research the target market's natural-language queries before choosing the angle.
- Separate primary intent from supporting questions. Do not force unrelated keywords into one page.
- Check the existing site and localized versions to avoid cannibalization and duplicate pages.
- Do not invent search volume, ranking, demand, popularity, or visitor numbers. Label third-party keyword volumes as estimates.

## 2. Title and search snippet

- Give every page a unique, descriptive `<title>` that states the destination/topic and the reader benefit or task.
- Put the most important natural search phrase near the beginning where it reads well. Keep titles concise; as an editorial target, aim for roughly 50–60 Latin characters, but judge final display width and clarity rather than treating a character count as a Google rule.
- Avoid vague titles, repeated brand phrases, keyword lists, clickbait, and unnecessary year/date additions.
- Write a unique meta description that accurately summarizes the page and gives a concrete reason to click. As an editorial target, aim for roughly 70–160 characters for Latin-script pages; Google may generate or truncate snippets differently by query, device, and width.
- For Japanese and Taiwan Traditional Chinese, write naturally in the target market's language. Do not translate English keywords word-for-word or add filler just to reach a character count.
- Keep the visible H1 aligned with the page title and primary intent. Do not repeat the title mechanically if a more useful H1 is clearer.
- Keep Open Graph and X/Twitter title and description metadata consistent with the page's actual content.

## 3. Article usefulness and structure

- Answer the reader's main question near the top; do not bury the practical answer under a long introduction.
- Use one clear H1, then logical H2 sections and H3 subsections without skipping heading levels.
- Include concrete decision-making details: who the option suits, where it is, how to get there, what to expect, likely constraints, alternatives, and what to verify before visiting.
- Use tables, lists, route steps, maps, FAQs, or comparison blocks only when they make the decision easier, especially on mobile.
- Add original local context, first-hand observations when genuinely available, and clearly labelled contributor tips. Never imply a personal visit that did not happen.
- Avoid padding, repeated summaries, generic travel prose, keyword stuffing, and thin pages written only to target a phrase.
- Add FAQs only when they answer real visitor questions not already answered clearly in the article.

## 4. Trust, facts, and sources

- Preserve exact Korean venue names alongside localized names so readers can find the correct place in map services.
- Verify business identity, branch, address, transport details, current operating status, menu/prices, booking rules, payment methods, and fees against reliable sources.
- Prefer official operators, government/tourism bodies, transport operators, and primary sources. Use reputable listings as secondary evidence and label them accurately.
- Record a checked date for volatile facts. If hours, prices, booking availability, or service details cannot be confirmed, say so plainly and tell readers what to recheck.
- Distinguish editor visit, contributor visit, local tip, public-listing information, and unverified lead. Do not turn a recommendation into a first-hand review.
- Link to primary sources for material claims. Do not copy another publisher's writing.

## 5. Images and mobile experience

- Use relevant, genuine, properly credited images; do not add decorative stock imagery that does not help the visitor.
- Write descriptive alt text for informative images. Use empty alt text for purely decorative images.
- Compress images, use responsive dimensions, and avoid layout shifts or oversized media.
- Put key answers, map links, directions, prices, and practical cautions where mobile readers can find them quickly.
- Check layouts at narrow mobile widths as well as desktop. Ensure links and controls are easy to tap.

## 6. Internal links and multilingual SEO

- Link to the most relevant neighborhood hub, transport guide, related attraction/food guide, and next useful planning step. Links must help the reader, not exist only to manipulate rankings.
- Keep canonical URLs self-referential and hreflang alternates reciprocal and accurate. Never point a localized page at a translation that does not exist.
- Keep localized navigation, breadcrumbs, search indexes, and sitemap entries consistent with the actual published pages.
- Do not machine-translate venue names, place names, or market-specific search phrases without review. Taiwan pages should read naturally in Taiwan Traditional Chinese; Japanese pages should use natural Japanese travel-search phrasing.
- Do not create separate language pages when the localized version would be thin or materially incomplete.

## 7. Structured data and technical release checks

- Use structured data only when it matches visible page content and the applicable Google feature guidelines. Do not add ratings, reviews, prices, opening hours, or FAQs that are not visibly supported.
- Validate JSON/JSON-LD, canonical, hreflang reciprocity, sitemap XML, internal links, image credits, and page status before release.
- Check for duplicate titles/descriptions, missing H1, broken links, orphan pages, accidental noindex, and incorrect canonical targets.
- Run the repository's build and SEO checks. Review mobile rendering and representative page speed/Core Web Vitals before production.
- A successful build is not proof of search performance. After publication, monitor Search Console impressions, clicks, CTR, query relevance, country, device, and index coverage.
- Do not request indexing in bulk as a substitute for fixing discovery, canonical, duplication, or content-quality problems.

## 8. Pre-publication checklist

- [ ] Primary search intent and target market are documented.
- [ ] Existing pages and localized equivalents checked for duplication/cannibalization.
- [ ] Unique title, meta description, and H1 match the page's actual content.
- [ ] Main answer appears early and the article contains useful, specific local detail.
- [ ] Venue names, addresses, routes, hours, prices, and booking claims are sourced or clearly qualified.
- [ ] First-hand status and contributor tips are represented honestly.
- [ ] Images are relevant, credited, optimized, and have appropriate alt text.
- [ ] Internal links help the reader complete the next step.
- [ ] Canonical, hreflang, sitemap, search index, structured data, and mobile layout pass checks.
- [ ] Build and SEO checks pass; Search Console monitoring owner/date is recorded.

## Measurement principle

The goal is not to maximize keyword repetition or publish the largest number of URLs. The goal is to earn relevant search impressions, convert them into qualified clicks, and help visitors make better Busan travel decisions. Prioritize pages with real Search Console demand, clear intent, and enough local information to satisfy the query.
